656 - no MathPix errors found; the whitespace class is mostly invisible
=======================================================================

Three things, all of them corrections to what this line of work has
been claiming.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. AFTER 155 EYE VERDICTS, ZERO RECOGNITION ERRORS

    0902.0431   75 rows + 60 rows   7 errors
    1510.06699  20 rows (blind)     4 errors  (+1 at n=60 earlier)
    ------------------------------------------------------------
    155 verdicts                   12 errors

EVERY ONE OF THE TWELVE WAS INKDRILL'S OWN PLACEMENT. Not one was a
MathPix recognition error. The red flags have never once found a
defect in the thing they were built to check.

`formularesidual` said "THAT DISAGREEMENT IS THE PRODUCT" on every
run. It is corrected in place to say what the labels say instead. That
line survived eight reports because nothing had ever been labelled.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE WHITESPACE CLASS IS REAL, AND THE INK CANNOT SEE IT

Measured on 2,048 ALIGNED rows -- same expression on both sides, so
the only question is the spacing:

    intra-token spaces, MathPix   2237
    intra-token spaces, author    1755      1.27x

    rows where MathPix has MORE space   455   22.2%
    rows where MathPix has LESS space   185    9.0%

    x y=y x              vs   xy = yx
    (a x) y+x(y a)       vs   (ax)y + x(ya)
    S U(3) \times        vs   SU(3) \times
    L_{e_{i}}=2 F_{i 0}  vs   L_{e_i} = 2F_{i0}
    s g s^{-1}=g         vs   sgs^{-1} = g

Exactly the class described: the maths is right and the reconstruction
of the spacing is not.

BUT A REPAIR CAN ONLY FIX WHAT THE INK CAN DECIDE, and in math mode
TeX IGNORES a space between two ordinary atoms. Each pair rendered and
the ink compared:

    x y        vs  xy           287 / 287   INVISIBLE
    S U(3)     vs  SU(3)        276 / 276   INVISIBLE
    F_{i 0}    vs  F_{i0}       144 / 144   INVISIBLE
    (a x) y    vs  (ax)y        484 / 484   INVISIBLE
    s g s^{-1} vs  sgs^{-1}     355 / 355   INVISIBLE
    { }^{t} A  vs  {}^tA        191 / 191   INVISIBLE
    \mid       vs  \, | \,      231 / 212   visible, +19
    \quad      vs  (nothing)    158 /  75   visible, +83

SIX OF EIGHT ARE THE SAME PICTURE. No measurement of the page can
choose between `xy` and `x y`, because the page does not contain the
difference. inkdrill cannot repair that class from the raster --
not badly, but at all.

SPLIT BY WHAT RENDERS, over the same 2,048 rows:

    EXPLICIT spacing (\quad, \, ...)   MathPix 17, author 50, 0.34x
                                       differs on 33 rows    1.6%
    TOKEN BOUNDARY (x y / xy)          MathPix 2237, author 1755
                                       differs on 640 rows   31.2%

So the repairable part is 1.6% and MathPix's error there is the
OPPOSITE direction -- it DROPS explicit spacing, using a third as many
`\quad` and `\,` as the author. The 31.2% needs the author's LaTeX or
a model of well-formed maths; the ink has nothing to say about it.

3. AND THAT CORRECTS out/648

out/648 ranked `{ }^{ }` prescript first among geometric substitutions
at +8.9% width, introduced in 320 of 333 documents. WRONG. That test
compared `\tau\left({ }^{t} A\right) A=E` against
`\tau({}^tA)A = E`, which ALSO differs in `\left(` against `(`. The
+39 px was the delimiter. Isolated, the prescript is 191 px either
way -- identical ink.

A pair that differs in two things measures neither. out/648 is
corrected in place, and its ranking is wrong at the top.

WHAT CHANGED IN THE CODE

  THE MARK IS SUPPRESSED WHERE THE PLACEMENT IS NOT TRUSTED. A
  rectangle in the wrong place is worse than none: it sends a reader
  to the wrong glyphs and looks authoritative doing it. No mark when

      gaps <= 1                       (nothing to place)
      margin < 0.10                   (the line repeats)
      gaps <= 2 AND edge_cuts >= 2    (wrong on 4 of 4, both documents)

  876 of 3,096 rows on 0902.0431 -- 28.3% -- now carry no rectangle.

  THE RESIDUAL REPORT IS THE PRODUCER'S OWN LOW CONFIDENCE. A new
  `LOW_CONFIDENCE` class at conf < 0.80: 161 rows on 0902.0431 (5.2%),
  103 on 1510.06699 (9.7%). That is what a reader should be asked to
  look at.

  THE RED FLAGS STAY, RELABELLED. They are evidence-table marks for
  someone chasing a detail, not a report. The tool now says so.

NOT CLAIMED

  * "NO RECOGNITION ERRORS" MEANS NONE FOUND IN 155 VERDICTS, all of
    them on rows this instrument had already selected. The instrument
    selects on placement fit, so it is not a sample of recognition at
    all. A recognition error that placed cleanly would never have
    reached a reviewer.
  * The whitespace measurement is on ALIGNED rows only -- 2,048 of
    5,643 -- which are the shorter and simpler ones by construction.
    The user's description names LARGE open expressions, and those are
    mostly in the unaligned 64%.
  * Source confidence does NOT predict placement error: nine of the
    twelve errors sit at conf 1.000. The two lists are independent,
    which is the reason for keeping them apart, not a reason to
    prefer one.
  * 0.80 is a chosen threshold, not a calibrated one.
