323 - the advisory, emitted as measurements
========================================================================

VALIDATION FIRST. 1511.08771 page 108, the row whose LaTeX carries
the 2 and the 1:

    \(\left(m \in \mathbb{Z} ; n \in \mathbb{Z}_{2 k+1}\right)\)
    is given by

    row: 25 glyphs, median glyph width 18.0 px,
         median gap 0.306 glyph widths

    after character  2 (x= 58): 0.94 glyph widths
    after character  3 (x= 98): 0.94
    after character  6 (x=154): 0.67
    after character  7 (x=191): 1.00   second mode
    after character  8 (x=232): 0.94
    after character 14 (x=375): 1.11   second mode   <-- THE GAP
    after character 17 (x=423): 1.00   second mode
    after character 23 (x=542): 1.00   second mode

THE 2 1 GAP IS 1.11 GLYPH WIDTHS, after character 14, against a row
median of 0.31. It falls in the second mode (1.00-1.25).

That is the same gap the task described as "1.12 glyph widths after
character 14", to within rounding, so the two measurements agree on
the same object.

WHAT 305 SAID ABOUT THE SAME GAP: 20 px against a 5.5 px median gap,
a ratio of 3.6, in a distribution where 84% of rows had something
above 3x. Indistinguishable from noise. The gap did not change; the
unit did.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE EMITTER

    python3 tools/rowspaces.py --pdf doc.pdf --lines doc.lines.json \
        --pages 108 [--floor 0.45] [--types math,text]

JSON. Per row of every named region: the median glyph width in px,
the median gap in glyph widths, and every gap at or above the floor
with its 1-indexed character position, its x in px, its size in glyph
widths, and whether it lies inside the measured second mode.

IT EMITS MEASUREMENTS, NOT VERDICTS. 306 was refused because "a space
is required at character 14" cannot be audited by its consumer -- an
LLM placing \quad has no way to tell a real space from an artefact.
"1.11 glyph widths after character 14, row median 0.31" is the same
fact with its evidence attached. A reader sees that 1.11 sits in the
upper mode and 0.31 does not, and can disagree with the floor without
re-running anything.

`in_second_mode` is a convenience flag, not the answer: the number is
beside it and the mode bounds are in the header of the JSON, so a
consumer that distrusts the flag has everything needed to ignore it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE FLOOR IS A REPORTING FLOOR

0.45 by default -- the trough 309 measured between the two modes,
where density falls from 10.8% to 3.3%. It decides what is PRINTED,
not what is true. `--floor 0` emits the whole distribution.

That distinction is why this is emittable and 306 was not. A
threshold that silently drops data is a claim; a threshold that
decides how much of a measurement to show, with the value attached to
every row that survives it, is a display choice the reader can undo.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
3. WHAT IS STILL NOT ESTABLISHED

That the second mode IS typeset spaces. It is in the JSON, under
`not_established`, so it travels with the data:

    the histogram shows two populations of gaps; naming the upper one
    is an inference from its position at one glyph width and its
    sharp right edge, not a measurement. Confirming it needs a join
    against the row's LaTeX, which this tool does not do.

Note what page 108 shows about this. Eight gaps in that row clear the
floor and four are inside the second mode -- but the row's LaTeX has
explicit spaces in `{2 k+1}` and around `\in`, and ORDINARY
interword spaces in "is given by". Both are typeset spaces; only
some are spaces a transcription must reproduce as `\quad`. The
measurement cannot tell those apart, and a consumer acting on it
without reading the surrounding LaTeX would insert spacing into
running text.

So the advisory says where the page has space. It does not say where
the LaTeX needs a command, and the JSON does not claim to.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
4. POPULATION

The mode bounds come from out/309.txt: one book, 1511.08771, 20 pages
sampled at fixed intervals, math and text regions, 202 rows, 7,894
gaps at 300 dpi. One typeface. A tighter setting moves the modes, and
the reference block in the JSON names the source so a reader can see
which measurement the floor came from.

RE-RUN: the command above. --floor 0 for the full distribution.
