497 - 495 re-run on the generated XITS Type 1 faces
====================================================================

THE QUESTION. 495 found \mathcal reading 1 skeleton junction on L, G
and J in Computer Modern and Latin Modern, and \mathscr reading 3 or
more in rsfs. But CM and LM are one drawing -- LM's cmsy is a
redrawing of CM's -- so the only independent test was txfonts, and
txfonts FAILED on G. A flat 1 measured on one family and its own
redrawing might be a property of \mathcal or a property of cmsy, and
495 could not tell which. STIX/XITS is the strongest available test
because it shares no lineage with CM: different designers, Times
proportions, delivered as OpenType and converted here by an entirely
different toolchain.

ANSWER: IT IS A PROPERTY OF \mathcal.

XITS ships BOTH forms in one font, which is the comparison CM cannot
supply -- cmsy has no chancery script and rsfs has no plain one. The
default script at U+1D49C is what unicode-math's \mathscr selects; a
`.cal` alternate, reached by the ss01 feature, is what \mathcal gives.
An OpenType feature cannot survive a .pfb conversion, but FontForge
kept the alternates as separate charstrings under suffixed names, so
both are readable.

  XITSMath-Regular, junctions / holes at 40, 80, 160 px

    glyph   \mathscr  (uni2112 etc)      \mathcal  (.cal)
      L      6/3   4/2   4/2              1/0   1/0   1/1
      G      3/1   3/1   5/2              1/0   1/1   1/0
      J      8/4   8/4   7/3              1/0   1/0   1/0

ONE FONT, ONE DESIGNER, TWO DRAWINGS, AND THE CHANNEL SEPARATES THEM
6-to-1, 3-to-1 and 8-to-1. The plain form reads 1 on every glyph at
every size, exactly as cmsy does. So the flat 1 is not an artefact of
Computer Modern; it is what a plain calligraphic capital IS under this
measurement, reproduced by a design with no connection to CM.

\mathcal ACROSS FOUR DESIGNS (junctions at 40, 80, 160)

    font        L            G            J
    CM        1, 1, 1      1, 1, 1      1, 1, 1
    LM        1, 1, 1      1, 1, 1      1, 1, 1
    txfonts   1, 1, 1      3, 2, 2      1, 1, 1     <-- the exception
    XITSMathR 1, 1, 1      1, 1, 1      1, 1, 1     <-- independent

\mathscr ACROSS FOUR

    rsfs10    4, 4, 4      3, 3, 3      5, 5, 8
    rsfs7     4, 4, 4      7, 3, 5      5, 5, 7
    XITSMathR 6, 4, 4      3, 3, 5      8, 8, 7
    XITSMath  4, 4, 4      3, 3, 3      5, 5, 5      (bold)

Every \mathscr reading is 3 or more; every \mathcal reading is 1
except txfonts G. The cut of 4 that 495 derived from that exception
still stands, and it is now derived against an independent family
rather than against a redrawing.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
A CORRECTION TO 495 SECTION 7

495's second addendum measured XITSMath-Bold, which carries only the
default script, and concluded that "the channel separates a PLAIN
script from a CHANCERY one as drawings" rather than two TeX commands
-- reasoning that Unicode has one script block, so \mathcal and
\mathscr are two fonts for the same codepoints.

The premise was right and the inference was wrong, because that
addendum had not seen the `.cal` alternates. XITS carries BOTH, and
maps them to the two commands the same way TeX does. So the drawing
distinction and the command distinction COINCIDE: \mathcal is drawn
plain in CM, LM and XITS, and \mathscr is drawn chancery in rsfs and
XITS. txfonts is the one family that draws \mathcal ornately, and it
is the one family the channel misreads.

That is a stronger result than the one it replaces, and it was
available only because the second conversion carried the alternates.
The error was reasoning from an absence -- "the Bold face has one
script block, therefore the distinction cannot be a command
distinction" -- when the absence was a property of the face converted,
not of the family.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
HOLE INSTABILITY, RE-MEASURED ON THE WIDER SET

    glyphs measured                       104   (was 57 in 495)
    HOLE count changes between 40/80/160   16   (15%, was 12%)
    junction count changes                 26   (25%, was 28%)

The rate did not fall as the font set trebled, and the XITS faces
contribute six of the sixteen. Two of them are \mathcal glyphs -- L
[0,1] and G [0,1] -- so even the flat form is not hole-stable: XITS's
calligraphic L closes a hole between 80 and 160 px while its junction
count stays at 1. The junction channel is the more stable of the two
HERE, which is the reverse of 495's overall finding and worth stating
as such rather than smoothing over: over all 104 glyphs junctions move
more often, but on the four \mathcal designs holes move and junctions
do not.

XITSMath-Regular's script is the least stable class measured: all four
of its glyphs change hole count. It is a regular weight, where the
Bold face moves three of four -- thinner strokes give more
near-tangent contacts, which is the same mechanism as everywhere else.

POPULATION AND RE-RUN

Six XITS .pfb files under ~/XITS, hand-converted from OpenType with
FontForge, plus the 16 TeX-tree fonts of 495. 22 font-block entries,
104 renderable combinations of 112, 4 glyph names, 3 sizes.

    INKDRILL_TYPE1=/usr/share/texmf-dist/fonts/type1 \
      python3 tools/famjunctions2.py out.json

TWO CONVERSION CAVEATS, both of which the harness now handles and
neither of which it can remove:

  The script block HAS HOLES. Unicode allocated eleven script letters
  to Letterlike Symbols before the plane-1 block existed and left
  their slots unassigned, so script L is U+2112 and script g is
  U+210A. A font can carry a complete script alphabet with nothing at
  U+1D4A7, and reading that as "absent" drops two of four glyphs from
  every script face. The harness now falls back to the Letterlike
  codepoint.

  XITSMath-Bold carries only BOLD variants and no `.cal` alternates,
  so every reading taken from it is a bold drawing compared against
  10 pt regular TeX faces. XITSMath-Regular removes that confound and
  is what the tables above use; the Bold rows are kept beside them so
  the confound can be seen rather than argued about.

STIX itself is still unmeasured. XITS is a fork of it and this is the
nearest available substitute, not the same font.
