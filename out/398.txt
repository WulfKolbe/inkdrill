398 — slivers dropped from each lattice BEFORE the two are paired
=================================================================

THE DEFECT

`compare` pairs A and B by index. When the two resolutions detect different
numbers of lattice rows, index i is a different row on each side, and every
row from that point on is displaced. The filtering that removed the extra
rows happened downstream, in `reportcompare` and in pdfdrill's `inkmeasure`,
which is too late: by then the pairing has already been made.

DTZ page 22, row heights in pixels:

  300 dpi   8 rows   49, 848, 3, 798, 3, 798, 3, 99
  600 dpi   5 rows   99, 1697, 1597, 1597, 198

The 300 dpi lattice finds three 3 px rules INSIDE figures that the 600 dpi
lattice does not. md row 2 was therefore A's 3 px rule against B's second
data row.

THE FLOOR, AND WHY IT IS NOT RELATIVE TO THE ROWS

A floor relative to the median row height does not work, and the reason is
the one 305 ran into: the divisor moves with the thing being measured.

  p22 @300   median row height 74 px   — smaller than every real row on the
                                         page, because the median is taken
                                         over a set half of which is slivers
  p22 @600   median row height 1597 px — no slivers at all on this side, so
                                         a median-relative floor would delete
                                         the 99 px header

PAGE HEIGHT does not move. 40 px at 300 dpi on a 297 mm page is 1.14% of it:

  SLIVER_FRAC = 40 / 3508

Every report in this corpus is 297 mm tall — A3 landscape and A4 portrait
share that dimension — so this reproduces the calibrated 40 px at 300 dpi and
scales itself at every other resolution without being told the dpi. Rows
below the floor are removed and the survivors RENUMBERED, which is the point:
leaving them in place with a marker would keep the two lattices disagreeing
about what index 2 is.

THE THREE PAGES

  p21   300 dpi 5 rows   600 dpi 5 rows   agree
  p22   300 dpi 5 rows   600 dpi 5 rows   agree
  p25   300 dpi 5 rows   600 dpi 5 rows   agree

p022 KEEPS ITS ROW. Re-measured the DTZ report end to end:

  before   132 TSV rows,  98 body,  p22 = 2 body rows
  after    134 TSV rows,  99 body,  p22 = 3 body rows

The three pages that remain off the 3-body pattern are correct: p1 and p34
are the short first and last pages, and p25 holds the genuinely empty data
row that 386 classed `absent`.

THE 12 REFUSED DOCUMENTS

Re-ran the 12 documents that the 322 pass refused with "compare returned N
rows, reportpages expects M" — the single largest cause of a refusal there.

  measured   10 of 12      520 rows recovered
  refused     2 of 12

And the two that still refuse do NOT refuse for this reason any more. Both
now reach the next check and fail it by exactly one row:

  preprints201901.0053.v1   run holds 19 data rows, manifest expects 20
  j.jcp.2018.03.019         run holds 37 data rows, manifest expects 38

Both were previously "page 3: compare returned 0 rows, reportpages expects
1". So the lattice disagreement is resolved on 12 of 12; two documents have
a different, downstream off-by-one that this task did not touch and should
not be folded into it.

THE COST, PAID

535 report.compare.tsv files are invalidated: every row index `compare`
emits has changed. At 4.6 s a page after 388 that is a re-measurement of
hours, not weeks.

ALSO IN THIS COMMIT

pdfdrill's own render path (`regionink._render`) was still png16m, so the
322 harness was paying the 93% decode that 388 removed from inkdrill's side.
Switched to pgmraw, with the rasters deleted as each page's compare
finishes — a PGM is uncompressed and a 400-page book would otherwise leave
tens of gigabytes of intermediates for a TSV of a few kilobytes.
