116 - rule context: what a rule DOES, from the ink around it
==============================================================

ANSWER FIRST, and it is mostly about the population. The test works
and all four classes fire. But on the Heim page it was asked for,
THERE ARE NO RULES TO CLASSIFY: `ink.rules[]` is empty on
tests/fixtures/heim_bh2_p96.png, 0 of 1,087 ink regions pass
`is_rule`. Six further Heim scan pages at 300 dpi yield 5 rules
between them. The distribution 116 asks for does not exist on a
scanned prose page, and the reason is the rule DETECTOR, not the
context test.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE TEST

`emit.rule_context(mask, rule, pt=, reach=1.0)` returns the ink
fraction in the band above and the band below one rule, where the
band is the rule's OWN x-span by `reach` x its own length in y,
clipped to the page.

Scaling the band to the rule rather than to a page constant is the
whole design. A fraction bar under a 12 pt numerator and a booktabs
rule spanning a 400 pt table need the same test, and any band
expressed in pixels or points fits one and not the other. Pinned by
a test that doubles every dimension and requires the coverages
unchanged.

It returns NO CLASS. `qc` sets the precedent: the cut between "ink
above" and "no ink above" is a threshold on a continuum, the caller
holds the page's own statistics, and a classifier here would fix
that cut for every caller. The four names are the caller's:

  fraction   above and below      overline   above only
  underline  below only           separator  neither

--------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE HEIM PAGE HAS NO RULES, AND THAT IS THE FINDING

  page                          ink regions   is_rule   free
  heim_bh2_p96 (the fixture)          1,087         0      0
  Massenformel p20                    3,250         0      0
  Massenformel p30                    1,775         3      2
  Massenformel p40                    2,706         0      0
  Massenformel p50                    2,100         0      0
  Massenformel p60                    2,051         2      2
  Massenformel p70                    2,170         0      0

`is_rule` requires fill > 0.8 AND a long side >= 20x the short one.
On these scans the aspect gate is what empties the population.
Solid horizontal bars (fill > 0.8) across the six Massenformel
pages, by aspect:

    aspect >= 20  (accepted)      3
    aspect 10-20                205
    aspect 6-10                   4
    aspect 4-6                    0
    aspect 3-4                   40

205 against 3. A scanned fraction bar is short and THICK -- ink
spread by the scan puts a 60 px bar at 5 px rather than 1 -- so it
lands at aspect 12 and is refused.

WHAT I AM NOT CLAIMING: that those 205 are missed rules. At aspect
10-20 a solid horizontal bar is also a hyphen, an em-dash, a minus
sign or the crossbar of a letter, and nothing here separates them.
The measurement says the gate excludes 98.6% of bar-shaped ink on a
scan; identifying what it excluded is a different measurement and
must happen before anyone moves the constant. Recorded as an open
question, not as a defect.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE DISTRIBUTION, WHERE RULES EXIST

Massenformel p30 -- 2 rules, both horizontal:

    y0_pt   x0_pt   len_px  above  below  class
     86.4   205.2      222  0.055  0.063  underline
    165.8   187.4      160  0.057  0.054  separator

READ THE COVERAGES, NOT THE CLASSES. All four bands sit between
0.054 and 0.063 -- body-text density, above and below, for both
rules. The widest gap in the sorted values is 0.0054, against 0.0024
and 0.0005 for the runners-up: no separation, so the cut the tool
derived is an artefact of sorting four nearly equal numbers. The
honest reading is that both rules have text on both sides and
coverage alone does not decide the class at this density.

Massenformel p60 -- 2 rules, both VERTICAL. Reported as vertical and
excluded rather than being given an above/below band that would lie
beside them.

arxiv 1408.0838 p8 -- 5 rules, all horizontal, and here the
coverages DO separate (7 of 10 bands exactly zero):

    y0_pt   x0_pt   len_px  above  below  class
    226.1   185.0       20  0.000  0.000  separator
    226.1   319.4       20  0.000  0.000  separator
    226.1   453.6       20  0.000  0.000  separator
    295.2   354.7       28  0.000  0.036  underline
    297.1   354.7       28  0.036  0.060  fraction

  cut 0.0179, the midpoint of a 0.0357 gap against 0.0242 and 0.0000
  for the runners-up -- a real separation, unlike p30's.

The three 20 px rules on one baseline with NOTHING within 20 px
above or below are not fraction bars; that geometry is three dashes
or minus signs in a row. The two 28 px rules 1.9 pt apart at the
same x are one object -- a double rule, or an equals sign passing
the aspect gate twice.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
4. `reach` IS LOAD-BEARING AND THERE IS NO STABLE ANSWER

Same page, same rules, band height varied:

    reach   fraction  overline  underline  separator
    0.5            0         1          1          3
    1.0            1         0          1          3
    2.0            2         0          0          3
    4.0            2         3          0          0

At reach 4 the three dashes become `overline`, because an 80 px band
reaches the PREVIOUS TEXT LINE. That is the argument for reach 1: it
asks whether something is attached to this rule, and anything larger
asks whether the rule is somewhere on a page with text on it.

The sensitivity is asserted in the suite so the argument cannot be
quietly dropped.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
5. FOR 118, IN ONE SENTENCE

pdfdrill asked for one grounded sentence for prompt variant E. This
is what 116 supports, and it is deliberately narrow:

    On this corpus the rule-context test locates rules whose bands
    are EMPTY on both sides -- 3 of 5 on arxiv 1408.0838 p8 -- which
    means a component the aspect gate accepted as a rule has no
    numerator and no denominator and is therefore a dash or a minus
    sign, not a fraction bar.

WHAT E MUST NOT SAY. It must not assert a missing fraction bar: 116
found none. The empty-band case is a rule that should not have been
called a rule, which is the opposite defect, and a prompt hint
claiming the opposite of the measurement is the fixture-to-answer
move 118 exists to avoid. If E needs a hint about missing structure,
it must wait for 117.
