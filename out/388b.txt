388b — reportcompare switched to pgmraw
=======================================

WHAT CHANGED

  tools/reportcompare.py   renders pgmraw, not png16m
  inkdrill/__main__.py     `compare` dispatches on suffix; .pgm/.pnm read
                           through pnmio, .png unchanged
  inkdrill/pnmio.py        `mask_from_pgm(path, threshold)` — a P5 file to an
                           InkMask with NO dpi
  inkdrill/__main__.py     `compare` emits the row's height as a new LAST
                           column, `row h`
  tools/reportcompare.py   the sliver filter reads that column instead of
                           re-deriving a lattice

MEASURED, one A3 page at 600 dpi, the real subprocess:

  before   60.65 s      after   4.6 s      13x

The decode was 93% of the page and is now 0.002 s/Mpx instead of ~0.7.
Lattice and five-tuple are unchanged and are now the whole cost.

NO DPI, AND WHY THAT IS NOT A HOLE IN THE CONTRACT

Every other pnmio entry point refuses without a dpi, for the reason its
header gives: PNM cannot record resolution and a mask silently in the wrong
coordinate space cannot be caught downstream. That rule protects callers
that convert to POINTS. `compare` is not one — it emits five counts per cell
and no geometry, and `__main__.py:559` already says so for the sibling
subcommand: "No dpi is required -- the five numbers are counts".

Inventing a dpi to satisfy a contract about lengths would put a fabricated
number in the one place that contract exists to keep honest. So the rule is
unchanged where it bites and the exception is named.

THE TRADE IS DISK, and it is handled

  same page   PNG 0.4 MB      PGM 66 MB      166x

So the rasters are no longer cached across runs: they are deleted as soon as
the compare that needs them has run. 34 pages at 132 MB a page would be
4.5 GB of intermediates for an output of a few kilobytes. Re-rendering costs
0.3 s, less than the PNG decode it replaces, so that cache never paid for
itself. Measured after a full 34-page run: 24 MB left on disk, the md files.

386's FIRST MECHANISM IS FIXED

The height now comes from the lattice that produced the row. reportcompare
was re-deriving a second lattice from a 300 dpi render and indexing it with
compare's row numbers; they agree only when both resolutions find the same
row count.

386's SECOND MECHANISM IS NOT, AND p022 STILL LOSES A ROW

Re-measured the DTZ report end to end. Still 98 body rows, and p022 still
has 2 where it should have 3.

The height fix cannot reach it. `compare` pairs the two lattices BY INDEX
when their counts differ (`comparing the first min`), and on p022 they do:

  p22 @300   8 rows   heights 49, 848, 3, 798, 3, 798, 3, 99
  p22 @600   5 rows   heights 99, 1697, 1597, 1597, 198

The 300 dpi lattice finds three 3 px rules inside figures that the 600 dpi
one does not. md row 2 is therefore A's 3 px rule against B's second data
row, and its height — correctly reported, from A's own lattice — is 3.

THE FIX, MEASURED BUT NOT APPLIED

Slivers must be dropped from EACH lattice before the two are paired. The
floor cannot be relative to the median row height: the median is contaminated
by the slivers themselves (74 px on a page whose real rows are 798), and on
p022 at 600 dpi there are no slivers at all so the median is 1597 and the
header would be dropped. That is 305's collapsing denominator again.

A floor proportional to PAGE HEIGHT works and needs no dpi — 40 px at 300 dpi
on A3 is 1.14% of the page:

  p21  300: 8 -> 5    600: 8 -> 5     agree
  p22  300: 8 -> 5    600: 5 -> 5     AGREE — the row comes back
  p25  300: 8 -> 5    600: 8 -> 5     agree

NOT APPLIED, deliberately. It renumbers every row `compare` emits, which
invalidates every md and every report.compare.tsv in the corpus — 535 of
them. That is a corpus-wide re-measurement, and it is the user's call, not a
side effect of a decoder switch.

1,109 tests pass, 2 new. Two existing tests failed first and were right to:
they asserted the `empty` column as `r[-1]`, which the new trailing column
displaced. A test that says "the last column" when it means "the empty
column" asserts the schema by accident, so both now index it by position.
