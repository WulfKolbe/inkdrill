PART 2 - ~/Downloads/inkdrill: blobscan IS CORRECT AND 21-295x SLOWER

A separate tree, no git, replacing the run-adjacency sweep INSIDE
`nest`/`ink_only` with `blobscan` -- a per-pixel neighbour case table
with coordinate stacks. Not the CR branch: no `_sweep_reference`, no
`attach`.

CORRECTNESS: EXACT. Over six real pages, 84.3 Mpx, the digest of every
ink region, every hole count and every nest region box:

    main (RAG)   8a2bcb3c02313a13b31a1913d886bc5c5669686fb068b26a3a272ff133e66df9
    blobscan     8a2bcb3c02313a13b31a1913d886bc5c5669686fb068b26a3a272ff133e66df9

That is more than BLOBSCAN.md claims. It says the hole counts agree
"on the fixtures", and it changes where they come from -- background
components' parentage instead of RAG cycle rank. On 84 Mpx of real
page they agree exactly. Its own suite is green, 1,203 tests, 38
skipped.

COST: THE ROUTE IS A DEAD END IN CPYTHON.

    page                        main (RAG)        blobscan     ratio
    sparse,  539 ink regions
      ink_only                  0.100s            29.488s      295x
      nest                      0.220s            29.453s      134x
    dense, 4,083 ink regions
      ink_only                  0.640s            32.531s       51x
      nest                      1.540s            32.451s       21x

AND THE SHAPE SAYS WHY. blobscan's throughput is 0.59 and 0.53 Mpx/s
on the two pages -- essentially CONSTANT, because it does the same
per-pixel work whatever is on the page. Main's is 174 and 27 Mpx/s --
it varies by 6x with content, because it is per-RUN and a run here
averages 40 pixels.

So the gap is WORST on sparse pages, which is most pages.

This is the measured form of what CLAUDE.md already records as the
reason for the mask encoding: `bytes.translate` and `bytes.find` do
the pixel scan in C. 629 measured that scan at 73 Mpx/s. A Python
per-pixel loop does 0.55. The factor between them is 130x, and it is
the whole of this result.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE 21x OF out/629

629 measured that everything above the pixel scan costs 21x the pixel
scan, and named the per-run bookkeeping -- 27.7 M function calls for
one page -- as the target. blobscan attacks that target by removing
the runs, and loses by one to two orders of magnitude, because it
replaces 380,000 per-run operations with 17,000,000 per-pixel ones.

The two results together bound the problem:

  * fewer operations per run (the CR branch)     -32.5% at NONE
  * no runs, per-pixel instead (blobscan)        21-295x WORSE

A 20x in CPython is not reachable by either. It needs the per-run work
to move out of the interpreter -- not to be rewritten inside it. The
one measurement that would say whether that is possible is what the
run extraction alone costs, and 629 has it: 73 Mpx/s against the
pipeline's 3.43.
