602 - the consumer, and the rect agrees with the lattice
====================================================================

THEY AGREE. 493 cells over five pages of two documents and three
tables of 4, 5 and 6 columns:

    edge   median   min   max   within 1 px
      x0      0.0     0     0        100%
      y0      0.0    -1     0        100%
      x1      0.0     0     0        100%
      y1      0.0    -1     0        100%

The x edges are EXACT on every cell. The y edges are 0 or -1 and never
+1, so the emitted rect ends at or inside the lattice cell and never
past it -- a rect that overran would crop the neighbouring row's ink.

So pdfdrill can emit against a consumer that exists and a test that
passes, which is what 602 was for. One thing changed in the spec on
the way, and one bug was caught before any emission.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE INDEPENDENCE, WHICH IS THE POINT

A checker fed a manifest derived from the lattice would agree by
construction and prove nothing. So the "emitted" side comes from the
PDF's OWN VECTOR CONTENT -- `mutool trace` reports the rules as
`stroke_path` at linewidth .3985, which is `\arrayrulewidth` -- and
that is the same geometry `\zsavepos` would report, read from the
typesetter's output rather than from the raster.

  Geometric_topology p20:  7 vertical rules, 22 full-width horizontal
                           -> 6 columns, 20 rows
  the lattice on the same page: 6 columns, 20 rows

Two detections, no shared code, same answer.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE BUG THE CHECKER CAUGHT, BEFORE 316,007 ROWS

The first consumer returned `floor(x) - 1` for the high bounds. The
checker reported:

    x1   median -1.0   min -1   max -1     over 60 cells

A CONSTANT -1 ON EVERY CELL OF EVERY ROW. `floor(x)` is already the
last pixel index at or inside x, so the further -1 was one too many;
`ceil` for the low bound and `floor` for the high one is the
half-open-to-closed conversion and needs nothing else.

That is the distinction the checker was built to draw and it drew it:
a SYSTEMATIC residual is a bug in the consumer and fixable; a
SCATTERED one would have been a real difference between the two
detections and a reason to change the spec. Reporting a per-edge
distribution rather than a pass is what made the two tellable apart.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
3. A CHANGE TO 601's FORMAT: THE RULE WIDTH IS NEEDED

601 specified `column_rules_bp`, `rule_above_bp`, `rule_below_bp` and
`page_height_bp`. Building the consumer showed a fifth field is
required.

THE CELL IS THE INTERIOR, NOT THE SPAN BETWEEN RULE CENTRES.
`_table_cells` reports the enclosed BACKGROUND region, and the
background begins where the rule ends, so the rect is inset by half a
rule on each side. At 0.4 pt and 300 dpi that is 0.83 px a side.
Dropping the inset moves five of the fifteen tests and is a systematic
error at every dpi.

    add   "rule_width_bp": 0.4

`\arrayrulewidth` is a document setting and the emitter knows it; the
consumer defaults to LaTeX's 0.4 pt when it is absent, which is right
for every report in this corpus and wrong for one that changes it
silently.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE CONSUMER

`inkdrill/cellrect.py`

    cell_rect(column_rules_bp, rule_above_bp, rule_below_bp, col, *,
              page_height_bp, dpi, rule_bp=0.4) -> Rect
    row_rects(...)  -> [Rect, ...]        every cell of one row

    Rect(x0, y0, x1, y1)   raster pixels, y DOWN, inclusive both ends
                           .width .height .is_empty

  G1  pure: numbers in, a Rect out. No file, no process, no raster --
      the geometry is the typesetter's, not measured from ink
  G2  input PDF user space (bp), y UP from the bottom; output raster
      px, y DOWN, inclusive, so a caller slices a mask with it
  G3  `page_height_bp` is REQUIRED, not defaulted: A4's height on an
      a3-landscape report moves every row by 1,452 px at 300 dpi, and
      the reports are a3 landscape
  G4  inset by half a rule; `rule_bp` an argument, LaTeX's default
  G5  column rules must ascend and `above > below`, both ASSERTED
      rather than silently sorted -- a caller with them backwards has
      a bug the rect would otherwise hide
  G6  an empty rect is returned as-is, never clamped: a zero-width
      cell is a finding about the emission

HELD. 15 tests, `tests/test_cellrect.py`. Seven mutants, all killed:

    restore the off-by-one              FAILED (5)
    drop the half-rule inset            FAILED (5)
    no y flip                           FAILED (5)
    ceil/floor swapped                  FAILED (3)
    drop the ascending guard            FAILED (1)
    drop the above/below guard          FAILED (1)
    dpi ignored                         FAILED (1)

Full suite 1,180, 37 skipped.

THE FIRST DRAFT OF THE TESTS ASSERTED MORE THAN THE MEASUREMENT SHOWED
and failed, which is worth recording. It asserted the y edges equal to
the lattice; the measurement says 0 or -1. The tests now pin the x
edges exactly, the y edges to the measured 1 px, and the SIGN of the y
residual separately -- three claims of different strength rather than
one tolerance standing in for all of them.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
5. THE CHECKER, AND WHAT TO RUN ON STAGE 2's FIRST OUTPUT

    python3 tools/pdfrules.py <report.pdf> <page>
    python3 tools/cellcheck.py <document> <page> --rows 30

`pdfrules` is the stand-in emitter and is throwaway once stage 2
exists. `cellcheck` is not: point it at a real manifest and it is the
acceptance test for the emission, per page, reported as a per-edge
distribution. Run it on ONE document before the corpus, and read the
shape of the residual rather than a pass -- section 2 is why.

WHAT IS NOT ESTABLISHED. Five pages of two documents, both LaTeX
reports built by the same builder at a3 landscape. A document whose
`\arrayrulewidth` differs, or whose rules are drawn as filled
rectangles rather than strokes, is not covered -- `pdfrules` reads
`stroke_path` only, and would return nothing rather than something
wrong, which is the right failure but is still a gap.
