117 - do the components a scan is MISSING sit next to a rule?
==============================================================

ANSWER: NO. The pooled figure says 17.2% against an 11.5% null and
looks like a 1.5x enrichment. It is not. Compared per row against
each row's OWN rule density, the deficit sits BELOW the null on 80
rows and above it on 29 (sign test z = -4.88, median difference
-0.056). The pooled number is carried by 16 rows, and with those
removed the rate is 7.6% against a null of 11.5% -- the opposite
direction.

There is no rule-adjacency signal in the deficit on this corpus.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT WAS ASKED AND WHAT COULD BE ANSWERED

117 asks how many of the MISSING components lie within one median
glyph width of a rule. That presumes the missing components can be
identified, which means matching the rendered cell's components to
the scan's.

THE MATCHING DOES NOT WORK, and this is a finding rather than an
implementation shortfall. I built it threshold-free on purpose -- each
component's box normalised to its own crop's ink bounding box, a
rendered component counted PRESENT when any scan component's
normalised box overlaps it, overlap `> 0`, no tolerance to tune. It
checks itself against the row's actual deficit:

    unmatched minus deficit    p5 -34    p50 -6    p95 +16
    rows where they agree to within 25% or 2:   61 of 426

Per-component correspondence between a LaTeX render and a SCAN of the
same expression is not recoverable by position. The two differ in
typeface, in scale, and in how ink decomposes -- a scanned `i` merges
its dot into its stem, one rendered mark becomes two scanned ones and
two become one. Any per-component answer would have been an artefact
of the matching, so no per-component answer is given.

WHAT REPLACED IT: zone counts, not identities. Each rule in the
rendered cell defines a zone, its box grown by one median glyph
width; the zone is normalised to the rendered cell's ink bounding box
and mapped onto the scan cell's. Both sides' components are counted
within the SAME normalised zone and the deficit is the difference. No
identity is claimed for any single mark.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE DENOMINATOR DECIDES, AND IT IS SMALL

    flagged rows where the render has more components than the scan
                                                            426
      with a rule in the RENDERED cell                      110
      with no rule at all -- cannot answer the question     316

74% of the population has no rule in the rendered cell. Those rows
are counted and named, not dropped: a measurement about rules over a
population that is three-quarters ruleless would be quoting a
subgroup as a corpus.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE POOLED NUMBER, AND WHY IT IS WRONG

    deficit falling in a rule zone      73 of 425      17.2%
    NULL: rendered components in a
      rule zone at all                 968 of 8,398    11.5%

The null model is the whole reason this is readable. Without it,
"17% of missing components are near a rule" reads as a finding; with
it, the question is whether 17.2% exceeds the share of ink that is
near a rule anyway.

It appears to. It does not survive being asked per row:

    per-row deficit share MINUS that row's own null share
      above its own null    29 rows
      below                 80 rows
      equal                  1 row
      median difference   -0.0569
      p10 -0.1765   p25 -0.1045   p50 -0.0569   p75 +0.0070   p90 +0.7447
      sign test, 109 non-tied rows:  z = -4.88

The pooled and the paired figures point in OPPOSITE directions. The
pooled one aggregates across rows with very different rule densities,
so rows that are rule-dense and deficit-heavy dominate it. Sixteen
rows carry it:

    16 rows where every missing component is in a rule zone
      they hold  44 of 425 deficit  (10%)
      they hold  44 of  73 near     (60%)
    the other 94 rows:  29 of 381 = 7.6%   against a null of 11.5%

Those 16 have a mean deficit of 2.75. A row with a rule and two
missing marks lands at 1.000 by arithmetic, not by evidence.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
4. WHAT THIS DOES NOT SAY

- It does not say rules are irrelevant to scan loss in general. It
  says that on THIS population -- 110 rows over 30 documents, the
  subset of my flagged negative-delta rows that have any rule in the
  rendered cell -- proximity to a rule does not predict where the
  deficit falls.
- It does not measure thin-ink survival. A rule that vanished
  ENTIRELY from the scan is a missing component near a rule only if
  another rule remains to define the zone. 116 already showed
  `is_rule` refuses most bar-shaped ink on scans (205 bars at aspect
  10-20 against 3 at >= 20), so a rule that survived thinner may not
  be counted as a rule at all. That is the measurement I would take
  next, and it is not this one.
- 0902.0431 contributes 2 of the 16 all-near rows. It is my largest
  document and its identifiers are withheld for the 16-page cache
  hole, so its rows appear here with id `?`. The zone measurement
  does not need an identifier, but a later join would.

5. FOR 118

The result is a NULL, and a prompt hint built on it would be
asserting a defect the measurement refuted rather than merely failed
