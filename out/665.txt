665 - the crop check re-run: a threshold that stopped holding, a shard lost to my own crash, and 461 wrong crops
===============================================================================================================

pdfdrill asked for a finished measurement before republishing. The marks
were ready; the STALE-CROP LIST WAS NOT, and checking why cost three of
my own mistakes. All three are here.

====================================================================
1. WHAT WAS READY, AND WHAT WAS NOT
====================================================================

READY. All 21 documents' marks, verified on 2026-09-13 by re-emitting
each one and comparing with the stored file: 0 refused, 0 readings
changed, every file reproduced exactly. 8,408 marks over 36,715 rows.
`index.json` and `table-rows.json` regenerated against the same build.

NOT READY. `stale-crops.json` was measured on 2026-09-11, against a
build that no longer exists. Re-checking its 159 ids on the 09-12
rebuild: 99 are now CORRECT (pdfdrill re-rendered them) and 60 still
wrong. Handing that file over would have had them re-render 99 crops
that are already right -- and, worse, would have missed the ones the
rebuild broke: the first finished shard of the re-run showed 10 wrong
crops in 0707.4470, a document that had ZERO on 09-11.

So the whole corpus was re-checked: 36,715 rows, 102 shards, ~40 min.

====================================================================
2. MY THREE MISTAKES, IN THE ORDER THEY WERE CAUGHT
====================================================================

a. A LOST SHARD, SILENTLY. `cropcheck.check` unpacked `int(r["page"])`
   for a row with no host line, and a row can have no page column
   either. One shard of mielke died, 101 of 102 files were written, and
   the aggregate looked complete. Found by counting output files against
   the job list, not by reading the totals. Fixed; the row is now
   reported as "no host line and no page column".

b. A TAXONOMY BUILT ON A FALSE PREMISE. I split the wrong crops into
   "right rectangle, wrong content" and "cut from another region" by
   comparing the crop's size with its host region -- assuming a crop is
   cut 1:1. It is not: a crop is scaled PER DOCUMENT, and the correct
   rows measure a single constant each (1.0 for eleven documents, 0.85
   mielke, 0.6 for four, 0.5 for two, 0.42 seven-sketches). Withdrawn
   and recomputed against each document's own constant.

c. A THRESHOLD QUOTED ACROSS A REBUILD. `WRONG = 0.35` was placed by
   eye on 09-11 inside an EMPTY interval: highest row judged wrong
   0.262, lowest judged right 0.413, nothing between. On the 09-12
   build that interval is full -- 599 rows lie between 0.30 and 0.50 --
   because the per-document scaling above pushes correct crops down: a
   0.42-scale, soft-printed crop of the right expression correlates
   0.35. Re-placed by eye on THIS build (section 3).

The general shape is the one HANDOVER already records: a result can be
stale against the artifact it was measured on. A THRESHOLD IS A RESULT.
Re-place it when the corpus is rebuilt, or it silently measures the
wrong thing.

====================================================================
3. THE CUT, RE-PLACED -- AND IT NO LONGER SEPARATES CLEANLY
====================================================================

30 rows examined by eye on this build, published crop against the
lossless cut of the row's host line:

    0.002 - 0.299   8 of 8 WRONG        a bold running head for its
                                        section line; a figure for its
                                        caption line; a neighbouring line
    0.302 - 0.344   5 of 6 RIGHT        blurred, reduced crops of the
                                        right expression
                    1 of 6 WRONG        johnston FO0086 at 0.304: the
                                        crop reads `e_2 \\in R^7`, the
                                        host line `e_3 \\in R^7`
    0.336 - 0.497  10 of 10 RIGHT       nine documents, all correct
    0.348 - 0.355   8 of 8 RIGHT

`WRONG = 0.30`: below every verified-right row (lowest 0.302) and above
the highest cleanly-verified wrong one (0.299).

IT IS A FLOOR, NOT A TOTAL. The two populations OVERLAP: one verified
wrong row sits at 0.304, above the cut. 15 of 16 rows examined between
0.30 and 0.50 are right, so contamination above the cut is roughly 1 in
16 near the boundary and falls off with height -- but it is not zero,
and no single threshold separates the classes on this build.

====================================================================
5. A THIRD OF THE RESIDUAL IS ABOUT TO DISAPPEAR BY YOUR OWN RULE
====================================================================

pdfdrill is moving to an additive structure where front matter, titles
and repeating frames are EXCLUDED from formula transclusion. Of the 461
wrong crops, the host line is:

    text                     220
    table_of_contents_item   115     <- excluded
    table                     83
    figure_label              31     <- excluded
    section_header            10     <- excluded
    table_of_contents_row      2     <- excluded

158 of 461 (34%) sit on lines that exclusion removes -- 60 of them in
0902.0431 (whose wrong crops cluster on its contents pages 2-4) and 43
in lyche. Re-rendering those is wasted work if the rows go away. The
83 hosted on `table` lines are the transclusion fault already reported
in `table-rows.json`, and they go the same way.

A useful order of work, then: exclude first, re-render second, and the
list shrinks by a third before anyone touches a crop.

====================================================================
WHAT THIS DOES NOT SAY
====================================================================

  * 461 is a FLOOR (section 3). Neither the false-negative rate above
    the cut nor the false-positive rate below it is measured; 30 eye
    verdicts, mine, not blind, are all that stand behind the boundary.
  * The 150 unattributed are not explained, only unexplained: no
    same-page region matches their size at the document's scale.
  * The host-line rule reproduces pdfdrill's own (first span in document
    order). Where the two differ -- 58 rows with no host line inkdrill
    can find, mostly a rewritten reading against MathPix's raw one --
    the row is skipped, not guessed.
  * This binds to a LINES.JSON-SHAPED FILE, not to MathPix: page
    records with typed line regions and their text. When pdfminer.six
    produces that file instead, every tool here reads it unchanged, and
    the `type` vocabulary is what the exclusion rule and the marking
    policy's "is this a line" clause both rest on.
