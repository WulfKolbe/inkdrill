WHAT STEP 0 CHANGED

`inkdrill/sweep.py`, 41 insertions, 2 deletions.

  * `sweep(..., moments: bool = False)`.
  * `SweepResult.moments: dict | None`, `compare=False, repr=False` --
    the same treatment as `_index`, and for the same reason: the flag
    must not be able to make two otherwise identical sweeps compare
    unequal, or the equivalence gate stops meaning anything at
    `moments=False`.
  * `moms_of` created in the loop and never written to.

`None` AND `{}` ARE DIFFERENT ANSWERS and step 0 pins both. `{}` is a
mask with no components -- complete and correct. `None` is "nobody
accumulated anything", which is the predicate step 4's fallback needs.
Collapsing them would make a blank page indistinguishable from an
unasked question, and `if result.moments:` is exactly the test that
would do it. Step 4 must use `is not None`.

`_sweep_reference` IS NOT TOUCHED. Verified mechanically rather than
by eye -- its function body is byte-identical to HEAD's:

    _sweep_reference identical to HEAD: True

That check exists because the first attempt at this edit FAILED it.
The block `uf = _UF()` ... `cycles_of = {}` appears in both functions,
so a plain textual replace hit the oracle as well and it raised
NameError on a keyword it does not have. Seven tests errored. This is
the same trap `tools/mutate_sweep.py` documents in its own docstring
-- "edits are applied to the last occurrence in the file, which is the
live sweep" -- and it caught me on the first edit of the CR. The
oracle-identity check is now part of how this step is verified.

THE HARNESS

`assert_moments(mask, axis, conn, *, fields=...)` compares
`sweep(moments=True).moments` against `moments_per_component`, root by
root and field by field, by NAME so a failure says which of the ten
integers is wrong. `assert_moments_all` runs both axes and both conns.
Step 1 will pass `fields=EXTENT_FIELDS`, step 2 the full ten.

EXACT INTEGER EQUALITY, stated in the docstring as a rule rather than
left to the reader. Every one of the ten is an integer by construction
-- `aggregate`'s G2 -- and that exactness is the whole reason this is
a move rather than a rewrite. An `assertAlmostEqual` in here would
hide precisely the reassociation bug the move could introduce.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
TESTS -- TestMomentsPlumbing, 4

  test_not_asking_yields_None_not_an_empty_dict          ok
  test_an_empty_mask_asked_for_moments_gives_an_empty_dict  ok
  test_the_flag_changes_nothing_else                     ok
  test_moments_match_the_oracle                          expected failure

The first three hold at EVERY step of B1 and will not be rewritten as
the accumulation lands. That is deliberate: asserting `moments == {}`
would pin a step-0 detail and have to be deleted in step 1, which is
churn masquerading as coverage.

`test_the_flag_changes_nothing_else` is the CR's central promise -- "a
caller that does not ask does not pay and does not notice" -- over the
whole fixture set, 276 comparisons. It compares nodes, components and
events FIELD BY FIELD rather than with `==`, because `moments` is
`compare=False` and `==` would pass even if the flag corrupted the
node list.

`test_moments_match_the_oracle` SHIPS FAILING, ON PURPOSE. The CR
offered "expected-to-fail, or simply do not call it", and warned
against a passing test that asserts nothing. `expectedFailure` is the
stronger of the two: an absent test gives no signal when step 1 lands,
whereas this one turns into an UNEXPECTED SUCCESS -- which unittest
reports as a suite failure -- the moment the accumulator is correct.
Step 1 cannot land quietly.

It runs on `comb`, a fixture with several components, NOT on the empty
mask: against an empty mask both sides are `{}` and it would pass for
the wrong reason.

NOT CLAIMED, AND ONE RISK

  * No measurement. Step 0 adds a branch-free `{} if moments else
    None` outside the loop; the per-run cost the CR is worried about

    removed in step 1 and would make the expectedFailure test an
    error rather than a failure.
