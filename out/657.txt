657 - the large expressions aligned, and the whitespace rate is FLAT
=====================================================================

out/656 measured whitespace on 2,048 exactly-aligned rows and closed
by saying those are the short ones, while the described defect lives
in large open expressions. This aligns the large ones and measures
there.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
THE ALIGNMENT, AND ITS SELF-CHECK

Exact matching cannot reach them and more rules will not help --
out/650 measured the marginal return collapsing, because a row aligns
only if EVERY difference in it is normalised at once.
`tools/fuzzyalign.py` matches on SIMILARITY instead: `difflib` over the
normalised strings, with a character-4-gram index to keep it out of
quadratic time.

WHITESPACE IS STRIPPED FOR THE MATCH AND MEASURED ON THE RAW FORMS.
If whitespace took part in the alignment, rows with unusual whitespace
would fail to align and the measurement would be taken on precisely
the rows that do not have the defect.

THE THRESHOLD IS CHECKED, NOT CHOSEN. Run against rows that ALREADY
align exactly, the fuzzy matcher recovers the same partner:

    0707.4470     92/92    1510.06699  204/204   2106.07890  152/152
    0902.0431   779/780    1605.05775   56/56    2501.06662  125/125
    2010.14265    66/66
    TOTAL      1474/1475   99.9%

    alignment rate by length      exact (out/650)   fuzzy
        50-99 characters                  ~10%      24.5%
       100-199                             ~7%      15.0%
       160+                                1.5%       ~14%

--------------------------------------------------------------------
PER ROW, THE DEFECT LOOKS LIKE IT GROWS WITH LENGTH

    length     rows  aligned   more space  less space  explicit differs
     0-24      2656     1060       26.0%        4.5%        0.8%
    25-49      1614      617       37.1%       17.2%        4.1%
    50-99      1024      251       43.0%       23.1%       11.2%
   100-199      334       50       54.0%       18.0%       16.0%

Twenty-six per cent to fifty-four. The explicit class -- the one the
ink can see -- goes 0.8% to 16.0%, twenty-fold.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
AND CONTROLLED FOR LENGTH IT DOES NOT

A longer expression has more places to differ, so "% of rows with at
least one difference" rises with length BY CONSTRUCTION. Per 100
characters of expression:

    length      n    expl MP  expl AU  ratio     tok MP  tok AU  ratio
     0-24    1057      0.01     0.11   0.12x       7.46    5.42  1.38x
    25-49     617      0.04     0.17   0.22x       5.36    4.01  1.34x
    50-99     251      0.10     0.16   0.63x       4.53    3.59  1.26x
   100-199     50      0.13     0.18   0.73x       4.16    3.15  1.32x

THE TOKEN-BOUNDARY RATIO IS FLAT: 1.38, 1.34, 1.26, 1.32. MathPix
inserts about 30% more intra-token spaces than the author at EVERY
length. The defect is real and it is constant per unit of content; it
does not concentrate in large expressions.

So the table above was measuring length, and my reading of it -- that
the defect grows -- was wrong for the same reason out/651's red-flag
enrichment was wrong. Third time in this file series.

WHAT IS TRUE PRACTICALLY: a long expression is much more likely to
carry AT LEAST ONE whitespace difference, 54% against 26%, and that is
what a reader sees. The per-token rate behind it is unchanged.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
THE REPAIRABLE CLASS RUNS THE OTHER WAY, AND IS WORST WHERE IT WAS
LEAST EXPECTED

Explicit spacing -- `\quad`, `\,`, `\;` -- is the part that RENDERS
and the only part the ink can adjudicate (out/656). MathPix does not
add it; it DROPS it:

    0-24 characters   0.12x the author's rate
    25-49             0.22x
    50-99             0.63x
    100-199           0.73x

WORST ON SHORT EXPRESSIONS. On a short inline expression MathPix emits
about an eighth of the explicit spacing the author wrote; by 100
characters it is at three quarters. That is the opposite of "problems
in large open expressions", and it is the class inkdrill can actually
repair, because a dropped `\quad` is 83 px of missing gap that the
page still shows.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THIS MEANS FOR A REPAIR

  REPAIRABLE, and worth doing: explicit spacing MathPix dropped. The
  ink carries the gap; the LaTeX does not. Short expressions are where
  the deficit is largest in ratio, long ones where it is largest in
  count.

  NOT REPAIRABLE from the raster: the 1.3x token-boundary excess, at
  any length. `xy` and `x y` are the same picture (out/656) and the
  page cannot be asked.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
NOT CLAIMED

  * ALIGNMENT IS STILL PARTIAL -- 15% at 100-199 characters, up from
    about 7%, and 1,980 of 5,643 rows overall. What the unaligned 65%
    do is unmeasured, and they are unaligned because they differ MORE,
    so they are not a random remainder.
  * The 0-24 band is not comparable with out/650's exact figures: the
    index skips keys under 6 characters, so the shortest expressions
    are absent here.
  * ratio >= 0.90 is one threshold. The self-check says it recovers
    known answers; it does not say what it accepts wrongly.
  * n=50 in the 100-199 band and 2 rows past 200, which is why the
    200+ row is dropped from the tables above rather than reported.
  * Seven documents, all arXiv, all LaTeX-produced.
