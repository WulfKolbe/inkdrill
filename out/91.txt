91 - why the region table detects five columns, not six
========================================================================

ANSWER: none of the four offered causes. No declared boundary is
invisible. The lattice was reading a DIFFERENT OBJECT -- an embedded
figure, not the table -- because `_table_cells` took "the ink region
with the most holes" and a picture inside a cell has more holes than
the frame has cells.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 1 - THE BOUNDARIES, DECLARED AND DETECTED

Document 0049, report page 3, rendered at 300 dpi (4961 x 3508 px,
A3 landscape). The region table is the third longtable in
report.tex:

    \begin{longtable}{|p{20mm}|p{7mm}|p{12mm}|p{78mm}|p{124mm}|p{123mm}|}
    Identifier | Page | Class | Source | Rendered | Scan

Each column occupies its declared width plus 2 x \tabcolsep
(6 pt each side = 4.23 mm). At 300 dpi, 1 mm = 11.811 px:

    declared   +2 tabcolsep   expected px   DETECTED px   error
      20 mm       24.23 mm         286          285       -1
       7 mm       11.23 mm         133          132       -1
      12 mm       16.23 mm         192          190       -2
      78 mm       82.23 mm         971          970       -1
     124 mm      128.23 mm        1514         1513       -1
     123 mm      127.23 mm        1503         1501       -2

All six declared boundaries have a detected counterpart, to within
two pixels. WHICH DECLARED BOUNDARY HAS NO COUNTERPART: none.

Before the fix the answer was not "five of six found" either. The
lattice returned six columns whose x-spans were
(2093,2247) (2217,2252) (2259,2295) (2271,2329) (2408,2553)
(2597,2634) -- overlapping, 35 to 154 px wide, all inside a 540 px
window on a 4961 px page. Not a table at all.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 2 - WHY

`_table_cells` chose the table as "the ink region with the most
holes". On page 3 the candidates are:

    holes  85   box   640 x  311   an embedded FIGURE      <- chosen
    holes  85   box   639 x  311   the other figure
    holes  74   box  4611 x 2662   THE TABLE FRAME
    holes  43   box   360 x  416   a third figure

The Rendered and Scan columns of a region table contain PICTURES, and
a picture's internal contours outnumber a frame's cells. The frame
has 74 holes for its cells; one figure has 85.

So the cause is not in the offered list -- not a missing rule, not a
rule under the detection floor, not merged columns, not a column
whose content misses its edges. It is the SELECTION of which region
is the table, made before any column is considered.

WHY IT SURVIVED UNTIL NOW: on the equation table the same rule is
safe by a wide margin. Page 2 of the same document has a frame with
145 holes and a runner-up with 2. Every table this project had
measured held formulas, and formulas do not out-hole their own
frame. The first table with pictures in it broke it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 3 - THE FIX, AND IT IS NOT A THRESHOLD

A table frame is the region its cells are HOLES IN, so it contains
every candidate competing with it. Largest bounding box is that
property, cheaply and with no constant to tune: 4611 x 2662 against
640 x 311 is two orders of magnitude, not a margin.

    p1   5 cols (declared 5)   unchanged
    p2   5 cols (declared 5)   unchanged
    p3   6 cols (declared 6)   was 6 spurious, now correct
    p4   6 cols, 1 row         was "no cells"

Page 4 -- the single-row page pdfdrill reported as undetectable --
now detects. That limit was a symptom of the same defect, not the
lattice's two-hole minimum.

WHICH SIDE WAS WRONG: mine. The report declares what it draws; the
detector was reading the wrong object.

`select` is now an argument, `area` or `holes`, because comparing
the two rules on IDENTICALLY FILTERED output is the only way to show
the change moves nothing else. See the corrected regression below.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 4 - BOTH TABLES, TWO DOCUMENTS EACH

    document      pages     table            declared   detected
    0049           1-2      equation             5          5
    0049           3-4      region               6          6
    2208.01506     1-10     equation             5          5
    2208.01506    11-12     region               6          6

Both shapes appear in one document in both cases, so a difference
between them is the table and not the build.

CORPUS REGRESSION, 25 documents x 2 pages, both selection rules
through identical filtering:

    pages compared   50
    unchanged        50
    CHANGED           0

The area rule changes nothing on the equation-table corpus. That is
the check against the failure this task named -- a detector that
finds six here and a different number next door.

A FIRST REGRESSION ATTEMPT SAID 47 OF 50 CHANGED, and it was wrong.
It compared raw `cell_grid` under the old rule against fully filtered
`_table_cells` under the new one; the width floor, containment and
content filters always reduce the count, so every page looked changed
whichever selection ran. `raw-old 7 -> filtered-new 6` was the
filters. Two sides of a comparison that are not the same quantity is
the defect this project keeps finding in other people's
measurements, and it was in mine.

TESTS

tests.test_emit.T1_21_TheTableIsTheLargestRegionNotTheHolliest --
three tests. A frame with a many-holed figure in one cell: the area
rule finds the frame (2 rows x 3 cols), the holes rule does NOT, and
an unknown `select` raises on a page that HAS candidates -- a blank
mask returns None before the selection is reached and would pass
whatever the guard did.

The fixture needed two corrections, both recorded in it: every cell
needs CONTENT or P15's column content test collapses the lattice to
one column, and the figure must be one CONNECTED mesh or it is 48
regions with one hole each and the frame still wins.

Suite 1,124 -> 1,127, all pass.
