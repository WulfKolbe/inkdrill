685 - one instrument sees one direction of failure
==================================================

out/683 ended with a self-report added to the paragraph gold: how far
down the source the walk reached, flagged under 60%. It was added
because pdfdrill's cross-check found that sigma26-077's gold stopped at
27.1% of its body and NOTHING SAID SO.

pdfdrill implemented the same check and then measured whether it would
have caught THEIR defect. It would not.

====================================================================
1. THE TWO DIRECTIONS
====================================================================

    mine    the walk STOPS EARLY.   `\end {pmatrix}` with a space was
            never matched, a depth counter never recovers, 83% of the
            document was skipped. Coverage 27.1%: far BELOW the healthy
            median of 88%.

    theirs  the walk NEVER SPLITS.  A blank line in a CRLF file is
            `\r\n\r\n` and their separator did not admit the `\r`, so a
            whole paper came back as ONE chunk. That chunk spans
            everything, so it scores ~100% coverage: ABOVE the healthy
            median.

A coverage check is a lower bound. It cannot flag the failure that
makes coverage look perfect, and the defect that produces it is worse
than the one it does catch -- a single enormous paragraph is still a
plausible-looking object, and theirs survived a full suite and a
downstream pipeline.

Their tell was the corpus looking wrong in the UNUSUAL direction: the
five CRLF documents held the five HIGHEST coverage figures, 99.0-99.7%.

====================================================================
2. THE COMPLEMENTARY CHECK, WITH THE FLOOR MEASURED
====================================================================

A body of several kilobytes is never one paragraph. So the second
instrument is the paragraph COUNT against the source SIZE, and the only
question is where the cut goes. Measured over 29 documents of this
corpus (20 SIGMA plus 9 arXiv):

    healthy, paragraphs per KB of source   min 0.52   median 1.51   max 4.50
    the whole body as a single chunk       at most 0.029

    PAR_SPLIT_FLOOR = 0.1

Five times below the lowest healthy document and three times above the
worst a single chunk can score. THE CUT IS IN THE MIDDLE OF THE GAP,
not on either edge -- the lesson from pdfdrill's gutter constant one
task ago, where 4.5% was the value a two-column page SHOWS and placing
the cut there would have detected nothing. A test asserts the floor lies
strictly between the two measured populations, so narrowing it toward
either edge fails.

A FIRST CANDIDATE WAS REJECTED BY ITS OWN MEASUREMENT. The obvious
signal is the longest paragraph as a fraction of the body. On this
corpus that reaches 60.8% in a HEALTHY document (sigma26-085, whose
last paragraph is followed by a long appendix), against 100% for the
defect -- a 1.6x gap with no room for a cut. Paragraphs-per-KB gives
18x. The rejected measurement is recorded because it is the more
instructive of the two.

====================================================================
3. WHAT WAS ASSERTED RATHER THAN LEFT TO LUCK
====================================================================

This walk is CRLF-safe, but only by accident of implementation: it
splits on `\n` and then calls `.strip()`, so the orphan `\r` is
whitespace and a blank line still reads as blank. Nothing asserted it.
`tests/test_paragraph_gold.py` now does, with a CRLF fixture -- the
defect that hit a peer is a test here whether or not this code could
ever have had it.

The `max(1.0, ...)` divisor survived its first mutation because no test
reached what it guards: a 0-byte source divides by zero and stops the
whole measurement rather than flagging one document. Asserted now.

====================================================================
WHAT THIS DOES NOT SAY
====================================================================

  * The floor is measured on 29 LaTeX documents from two publishers. A
    corpus of one-paragraph notes would sit below it legitimately; the
    divisor floors at 1 KB so a short file cannot trip it, which is a
    patch on that, not a solution.
  * Neither check verifies that the paragraphs are RIGHT. They are
    cheap guards against a whole class of silent instrument failure,
    and nothing more.
  * Two instruments cover two known directions. There is no argument
    here that they are the only two.
