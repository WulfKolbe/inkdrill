====================================================================

POPULATION for all three: three real pages chosen for SEPARATED ink
density, 600x600 crops at 300 dpi taken at each page's densest window
(so a result is not a crop of the margin), threshold 200.

    2002.06055 p6    11.34% ink in the crop
    1205.3463v2 p3    8.60%
    2005.05886 p5    13.90%

A2 - THE PREMISE IS REFUTED: THERE IS NO CYCLE GROWTH TO EXPLAIN

  *** NARROWED BY 589. This section is true of RENDERED pages at 300
  dpi and false of SCANS below it. warp.py's table was measured on
  DocReal scans; this was measured on rendered arXiv pages, which is a
  population difference and not a disagreement about the code. On
  DocReal at threshold 200 transport grows cycles on 0% of crops at
  600 dpi, 30% at 300 and 78% at 120. See out/589.txt. ***

The task asks whether the growth is `_fill_quad`'s scanline rounding
or geometry. It is neither, because transport does not grow cycles.
It SHRINKS them, on every page and at every angle tried:

  900x900, 7 deg    source      transport   dilate(tr)  dilate(src)
  2002.06055     (527, 162)    (411, 127)   (288, 162)   (398, 197)
  1205.3463v2    (596, 194)    (574, 142)   (394, 344)   (507, 417)
  2005.05886     (862, 304)    (749, 234)   (446, 470)   (601, 590)

  same at 3 deg and 15 deg, and at 600x600.

The numbers the premise comes from are in warp.py's own docstring --
(436, 180) -> (408, 2238), an order of magnitude of new holes. Those
are the PRE-FIX measurement. The docstring says so, under a heading
reading "FIXED: transport used to hatch solid regions", and then
prints the table of the defect it fixed. The fix is the quadrilateral
fill in `_fill_quad`, and it works: a run is transported as its pixel
area, neighbouring runs still touch, and the hatching is gone.

The dilation control matters and is reported above rather than
skipped. Dilating closes real holes as well as any rounding gap, so
dilate(transport) against RAW source would credit the fill for holes
it merely filled. Against dilate(source) -- the paired comparison --
transport has FEWER cycles at every page: 162 vs 197, 344 vs 417,
470 vs 590. There is no gap population to find.

WHAT SHOULD CHANGE: the docstring, not the code. A "FIXED" heading
above a table of the broken behaviour reads as current, and it was
read that way. The measurement above belongs beside it.

A1 - THE CROSSOVER IS PER CHANNEL, AND ONE PAGE INDEED CANNOT FIND IT

Which path is nearer the source, by channel, over 12 angles:

  angle        2002.06055        1205.3463v2       2005.05886
              comp    cyc       comp    cyc       comp    cyc
   0.5        res     TR        TR      TR        TR      TR
   1          TR      TR        TR      TR        TR      TR
   3          res     TR        TR      TR        res     TR
   7          TR      TR        TR      TR        res     TR
  10          TR      TR        res     TR        res     TR
  15          res     TR        res     TR        res     TR
  20          res     res       res     TR        res     TR
  30          res     TR        res     res       res     TR
  45          TR      res       res     res       res     TR

  CROSSOVER on cycles   15-20 deg      20-30 deg       none by 45

ON THE CYCLES CHANNEL TRANSPORT IS NEARER ALMOST EVERYWHERE, and the
angle at which resample overtakes it is 15-20 deg, 20-30 deg, and
beyond the swept range -- three different answers from three pages,
which is the task's own point and now measured rather than asserted.
It does not order with ink density (11.34% -> ~17 deg, 8.60% -> ~25,
13.90% -> >45), so density is not the variable; something else about
the ink is, and this does not say what.

ON THE COMPONENTS CHANNEL RESAMPLE IS USUALLY NEARER. So the common
verdict is SPLIT, which is G7 being right: a single boolean would have
had to pick a channel, and `max()` would have picked whichever was
worse.

THE THESIS IS VISIBLE ON ONE PAGE. 1205.3463v2 at 0.5 deg:

    source     (272,  70)
    transport  (268,  66)      drift 0.015, 0.057
    resample   (320,  57)      drift 0.150, 0.186

Resample GAINS 48 components against a source of 272 -- thin strokes
broken into pieces, which is precisely the failure the unit was built
to demonstrate and which the synthetic fixtures never produced. On the
other two pages resample does not fragment and the two paths sit
within a few percent of each other.

A DEFECT FOUND WHILE SWEEPING: THERE IS NO TIE STATE. At 0 degrees
both paths are exact -- drift (0.0, 0.0) each -- and
`nearer_by_channel` compares with strict `<`, so both channels read
False, `transport_is_nearer` returns False, and the harness reports
that RESAMPLE is nearer at the identity transform. G5 says the
identity is a fixed point of transport; the verdict says transport
lost. Not a wrong number, a missing third value: `nearer_by_channel`
has no way to say "equal". Reported, not changed -- fixing a contract
property is not a report-only task.

A3 - GROUPING IS THE MISSING PIECE, AND THE MARGIN IS TWO ORDERS

Real page ink rotated by a KNOWN angle, so the truth is exact rather
than estimated. Blobs grouped by union-find over box gaps <= 6 px
(box distance, not centroid: two fragments of one broken stroke face
each other along an edge and can be far apart centre to centre).

  skew +3.0 deg applied      blobs -> groups   per BLOB      per GROUP
                                               median  IQR   median  IQR
  2002.06055                   279 -> 56        0.00 171.57   -2.42 3.09
  1205.3463v2                  242 -> 69      -13.55  90.00   -2.86 3.47
  2005.05886                   395 -> 85       -3.58 173.16   -3.01 3.24

PER BLOB THE ANGLE IS NOISE: an interquartile range of 90 to 172
degrees on a 180-degree scale is very nearly uniform. A single glyph
has no orientation to measure -- min-area-rect returns the angle of
whichever hull edge happens to minimise area, and for a letter that is
arbitrary. PER GROUP the IQR is about 3 degrees and the median tracks
the applied skew.

Linear across applied angles, and 0 is a clean fixed point:

    applied     recovered (three pages)
     -3.0      +2.83  +2.21  +2.86
      0.0       0.00   0.00   0.00      (IQR 0.72, 0.00, 0.00)
     +3.0      -2.42  -2.86  -3.01
     +6.0      -5.54  -5.96  -6.01

Worst error 0.46 deg, best 0.01 deg.

SIGN: recovered = -applied, and both are right. `rot()` is built in
the package's row-vector order on image coordinates, where positive is
counter-clockwise ON SCREEN; `min_area_rect` reports `atan2` in those
same image coordinates, which is the opposite sense. Named here rather
than silently flipped -- the core stores no angles precisely so that
each producer has to say which convention it is in.

AND THE GROUP ANGLE IS NOT AN AVERAGE OF ITS MEMBERS. Within-group
member spread has a median of 88 to 160 degrees. Averaging blob angles
would reproduce the noise; the group's union has to be re-fitted, and
that is what the numbers above do.

the file and are printed with their measured ink fraction on every
run.

NOT ESTABLISHED. What the crossover actually tracks, given it is not
ink density. Whether the grouping survives a page with real rather
than applied skew -- applied rotation gives exact truth and cannot
show what a scanner's own distortion does. And the tie state in
`nearer_by_channel`, which is a contract change and belongs to whoever
owns G7.
