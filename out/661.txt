661 - the junction angle: MathPix's harpoon readings are floors in the ink, on all 15 measured
==============================================================================================

pdfdrill's census (their out/682) found 30 instances where the ink is
nearly identical and the junction angle is not: `⌋` (`\rfloor`) square
against `⇃` (`\downharpoonleft`), an acute barb. Component counts, hole
counts and ink distance all pass them, and on a SCAN there is no text
layer and no `/Encoding` to appeal to. This is the ink half: can the
angle be read off a real page, and does it separate?

    python3 tools/glyphangle.py reference
    python3 tools/glyphangle.py document "<mielke>" --out F.json --crops D

====================================================================
1. THE FEATURE
====================================================================

Two numbers, both read off the per-row spans of the run adjacency:

    angle   the arm against the stem. 90 = a horizontal foot.
    drift   how far the arm's outer edge moves per scan row.

The STEM IS FOUND -- the widest contiguous band of columns carrying ink
in at least 60% of the glyph's rows -- and the arm is what reaches past
it at the bottom. Not the extreme columns: that is direction-blind and
read a mirrored `⌊` as a `⌋`. See section 6.

NOT FROM A SKELETON. A thinned skeleton gives the same angle in
principle, and 496 measured 26% of glyphs changing their junction count
with size; the instability would be inherited for nothing.

====================================================================
2. THE CONTROL: the glyphs themselves, at the sizes real ink comes in
====================================================================

Rendered through U9's chain (type1 -> charstring -> scan), which is why
this could be checked against ground truth at all:

    em px   ⌋ cmsy10/floorright          ⇃ msam10/harpoondownleft
            angle  drift  arm   h        angle  drift  arm   h
      34     90.0   0.0    1    34        59.0  1.33    4   30
      40     83.7   0.0    2    40        50.2  1.00    6   35
      46     84.3   0.0    2    46        58.0  1.20    6   41
      52     85.2   0.0    2    52        56.3  1.17    7   46
      58     85.6   0.0    2    58        51.3  1.00    9   52
      64     82.4   1.0    3    64        57.5  1.14    8   57
      80     84.0   0.5    3    80        54.5  1.10   11   71

THE ANGLE SEPARATES AT EVERY SIZE -- 23 to 33 degrees apart, never
closer. THE DRIFT DOES NOT: at 64 px the floor's own foot drifts 1.0,
which is what the barb drifts. A rule resting on the drift would fail
there, and the first draft of this file rested on it (section 6).

At 34 px the foot is ONE row and the angle saturates at 90; below about
40 px the feature stops being readable, so `stem_arm` returns the
height and the caller decides (MIN_HEIGHT = 40).

====================================================================
3. THE INK, in the one book that reads the same glyph both ways
====================================================================

mielke-geometrodynamics uses `⌋` as the INTERIOR PRODUCT, an operator,
not a delimiter: 75 of its rows carry `\rfloor` with no `\lfloor`, where
every other document in the corpus is balanced (31 rows, both halves).
Every harpoon reading in all 21 documents is in this book. So the two
populations share a page, a font, a rasterisation and a dpi -- a control
group no synthetic fixture can match.

71 glyph instances measured on the LOSSLESS 400 dpi page:

    MathPix's reading   n   places   angle          drift   arm   height
    \rfloor            56     47     79.7 - 80.5     0.0     3    40 - 47
    \downharpoonleft   15     11     80.5            0.0     3    47

EVERY instance read as a harpoon measures as a floor, and not near the
boundary: 80.5 degrees where a real barb at that size reads 50-58, and
a drift of 0.0 where a real barb reads 1.0-1.33. The two label groups
are not merely close -- they are identical, to the decimal.

"places" counts distinct (page, line): the 15 are spread over 11 of
them, not one line counted many times.

LOOKED AT BY EYE, twice: 24 glyphs from the first extraction and 12
from the corrected one (8 harpoon-read, 4 floor-read), enlarged 4x and
set side by side. Every one is the same glyph -- a stem with a flat
foot -- and the harpoon-read ones cannot be told from the floor-read
ones. `--crops` writes them, so this is re-checkable.

====================================================================
4. WHAT WAS NOT MEASURED, AND WHY
====================================================================

87 places carry a symbol (57 display lines, 30 inline rows). The two
passes dropped 87 occurrences:

    48  the component under the mapped position is not a stem and arm
    32  no component landed at the mapped position
     4  the locator found a different number of stem-and-arm candidates
        than the LaTeX has -- other glyphs of that shape (`£`, `]`, `L`)
        are candidates too
     2  would not render at all
     1  no host line

A drop is silence, not evidence. It also means the 71 are not a random
sample: they are the ones the placement could reach, and a systematic
difference between reached and unreached occurrences is not ruled out.

THIS IS A BORN-DIGITAL PDF. One glyph renders to an identical bitmap
everywhere, which is why the numbers repeat exactly rather than spread.
On a scan they will spread, and the threshold has to be set on a scan
population before it can be used there. The separation is wide, so
there is room; that is an expectation, not a measurement.

A SIDE FINDING. 2 of the 57 display lines cannot be rendered because
MathPix emitted the Arabic letter ل (U+0644) inside a physics formula.
A different defect, in the published corpus now.

====================================================================
5. THE RULE THIS SUPPORTS -- a residual, not a verdict
====================================================================

    the reading says harpoon AND the ink stands square
    (angle > 75 deg, height >= 40 px)

On this book that fires on 15 instances and on none of the 56 floors.
The drift is reported beside it and is NOT part of the test, because
section 2 shows it stops separating at 64 px.

NOT in the marking policy, and nothing is auto-corrected: the finding
is a disagreement between the producer and the ink, reported with both
numbers, exactly as 658 settled for the placement flags.

It generalises the way pdfdrill's census says: per component, the arms
at each stem terminal with angle, length and drift, plus the corner's
position along the stem. `+` vs `×` is the angle alone; `⊥` vs `⊢` is
the corner position; `/` vs `↗` is the arm count at one end.

====================================================================
6. THE HARNESS, AND THE TWO DEFECTS ITS TESTS FOUND
====================================================================

`tools/glyphangle.py` -- `reference` (section 2) and `document
<bibkey>` (section 3, with `--crops` writing each measured glyph for
the eye). `tests/test_glyphangle.py` pins it on the REAL glyphs, both
rendered once at 46 px and pasted in as fixtures.

Three versions of this measurement were wrong before it was written up,
and each was caught by a test rather than by reading the code:

  a. A HAND-DRAWN barb, whose reach and row count were chosen by me,
     failed its own separation test. Fixture dimensions from nowhere;
     replaced by the rendered glyphs.
  b. `stem_arm` took the extreme columns as stem and tip, so it was
     DIRECTION-BLIND: a mirrored `⌊` measured exactly as a `⌋`, and the
     docstring's "reaches left" was prose. The stem is now found. The
     corrected version measures MORE of the barb (6 arm rows at 46 px
     against 4) and separates wider.
  c. The separation was claimed on the drift. The opt-in size sweep
     showed the floor drifting 1.0 at 64 px -- the same as the barb --
     so the rule was moved onto the angle and a test now RECORDS the
     drift's failure rather than hiding it.

NOT DONE. The overprint/containment test (bounding-box containment as
evidence of an overlay) is pdfdrill's second proposal and is NOT
measured here; it is next, on this same document.
