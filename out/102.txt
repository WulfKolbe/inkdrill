--------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE TEST

`mathstruct.region_overrun`, two conditions, both required:

  SEPARATED  outside the main ink's single-linkage cluster at
             `gap_scale` x the crop's MEDIAN COMPONENT WIDTH.
             Relative to the crop's own glyphs, so a dpi change
             cannot silently retune it.
  ANCHORED   where the cluster sits. Two readings, and they are
             DIFFERENT STATEMENTS rather than one statement at two
             tolerances:
               edge     some member reaches within `edge_tol` px of
                        a crop border  (what 102 specified)
               extreme  the cluster is the outermost in some
                        direction - no cluster reaches further left,
                        right, up or down

Judged per CLUSTER, not per component: ink that crossed a boundary
is cut by it and arrives in pieces, one blob touching the border and
the rest just inside, so judging each piece alone keeps the interior
halves and still reports a surplus.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
3. WHY `edge` FAILS, AND IT IS NOT A TOLERANCE

  margin between the ink and the crop border, over 1,665 rows
    left    min 0   p50  29   p95   32   max   92
    top     min 0   p50   9   p95   13   max   28
    right   min 53  p50 604   p95 1795   max 1900
    bottom  min 0   p50 176   p95  723   max 1473

My crop is the report's Scan CELL, and a longtable cell is sized by
the longest row in its column. On a short equation the pasted scan
image floats in a much larger box: the median row has 604 px of
empty cell to the right of its ink and the widest has 1,900. A
condition asking whether ink reaches the crop border is therefore
measuring CELL LAYOUT, not the region boundary - a real number about
the wrong subject, which is the same failure as a threshold
expressed in page pixels.

Only 36 of 1,666 rows have ANY component touching a border at all.
That ceiling is why no `edge_tol` rescues it: at tol 4 the condition
starts admitting rows whose ink merely runs close to the cell edge,
which is a different mistake, not a fix.

pdfdrill found the same defect from the other side, on the raw crop
JPEGs: the rightmost ink stopped 6, 6, 6 and 7 px short of the
border on four crops of different sizes from two documents. A
CONSTANT margin across differently sized crops is the tell - it is
the producer's inset, not where the content ends. Their inset is 6
px; mine is 604.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
5. THE NUMBER I WOULD DEFEND: 14

Firing is not evidence. The test that matters is whether the
explanation EXPLAINS: remove the alleged overrun components,
recompute the five-tuple on what is left, and ask whether the row
falls back inside the measured noise band (distance <= 7, component
delta <= 2).

  gap_scale        0.5    1.0    1.5    2.0    3.0
  fires           1590   1221    860    587    315
  surplus count
    explained      654    621    456    323    191
  FALLS TO NOISE     9     14     22     16      7

At the natural setting, 621 rows have their surplus COUNT
arithmetically explained by the removed components and 14 become
unremarkable once those components are gone.

That gap of 607 is the real result. The removed components account
for HOW MANY marks differ and not for HOW the row differs: the
arithmetic balances and the five-tuple still separates. So where
overrun is present it is usually ACCOMPANIED BY a genuine
difference rather than being the whole of it. That is a claim about
the composition of the class, and it is a stronger negative than a
small percentage would have been - a small percentage would only
have said overrun is rare.
