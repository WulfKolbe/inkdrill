309 - re-normalised by median glyph width: a separation DOES appear
========================================================================

ANSWER: yes. The width-normalised gap distribution is BIMODAL, with
a trough between the modes at about 0.45 glyph widths. The channel is
not closed and 306 becomes buildable -- but the separating threshold
is about HALF a glyph width, not "several times the median", so the
rule 305 and 306 were written around is the wrong shape even though
the signal is there.

FIRST, A CORRECTION TO out/305.txt. Section 4 of that file explains
the failure by saying "the row median gap is 1.00 px on half the rows
measured". THAT IS FALSE, and this run measured it directly:

    rows whose median gap is <= 1 px      0 of 202
    median GAP per row      p10 3.0   p50 5.0   p90 57.0
    median WIDTH per row    p10 14.0  p50 18.0  p90 20.0

I mistook the RATIO's p50 of 1.00 for the DENOMINATOR being 1. A
ratio's median is 1.00 by construction -- half of any row's gaps lie
below that row's own median -- and I built a mechanism on top of a
tautology. The measure does fail; the reason I gave for it was wrong.

2. AGAINST 305, ON THE SAME ROWS

Only the denominator differs, so the difference is the normaliser.

    threshold   305 gap/median-GAP   309 gap/median-WIDTH
      >= 1.5x        89.6%                 76.2%
      >= 2x          87.6%                 54.0%
      >= 3x          84.2%                 35.6%
      >= 5x          70.3%                 23.3%
      >= 8x          49.0%                 19.8%
      >= 10x         36.1%                 18.3%

    row-count drop between successive cuts
      305    21  4  7 10 18 13 30 26      still falling at the top
      309    43 45 37 16  9  1  6  3      front-loaded, then flat

305 declines steadily to the end: no body, no tail, no cut. 309 has
both, and the plateau from 5x onward loses 10 rows across four
thresholds against 82 rows between 1.5x and 3x.

BUT THE ROW COUNTS ARE NOT THE FINDING. A plateau in rows can come
from a few rows holding many large gaps, which would be one row
population and not two gap populations. The histogram in section 1 is
what separates those readings, and it is the reason this answer is
"yes" rather than "suggestive".

--------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE THRESHOLD IS NOT WHERE THE TASK EXPECTED IT

305 and 306 were written around "a gap several times the median". On
this normaliser the cut is at about 0.45 -- LESS THAN HALF a glyph
width -- and the space population sits at roughly one glyph width.

That is the right magnitude for the thing being detected. An
interword space is about the width of a character; it is not several
characters wide. The original framing asked for a multiple, and the
answer is a fraction, because the denominator changed from something
tiny (a 5 px inter-glyph gap) to something the size of the object
itself (an 18 px glyph).

Reading it as "several times the median gap" would have put the cut
at 5x-10x, which on this distribution is 18-23% of rows and lands
deep in the tail past the second mode -- catching the very largest
gaps and missing the space population entirely.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
4. WHAT IS NOT ESTABLISHED

That the second mode IS typeset spaces. The histogram shows two
populations of GAPS; calling the upper one "spaces" is an inference
from its position (one glyph width) and its hard right edge (a fixed
character), not a measurement.

CONFIRMING IT NEEDS A JOIN: take the gaps above 0.45 in a row, and
check that their character positions correspond to positions where
that row's LaTeX has an explicit space. That is exactly what 306
would emit, so it must be verified BEFORE 306 is built and not by
building it -- otherwise the emission is its own evidence.

The same caution as 246: three statistics at the 94th percentile
would have separated one diagram from 75 equations and I refused to
build on it. Here there are 7,894 gaps and a visible trough, which is
a stronger footing -- but it is still one book, one typeface, and no
labelled positive.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
5. POPULATION

1511.08771, 20 pages sampled at fixed intervals, `math` and `text`
regions only, 202 rows, 7,894 gaps, 300 dpi. Same rows as 305 so the
two normalisers are compared on identical input. One book and one
typeface; a tighter setting would move the mode positions and nothing
here claims otherwise.

STATUS OF 306: no longer refused on the grounds 306 gave -- the
signal exists. Still not built, pending section 4's join, and its
threshold must be restated as a fraction of glyph width rather than a
multiple of the median gap.
