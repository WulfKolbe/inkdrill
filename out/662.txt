662 - bounding-box containment does NOT find overprints on a raster: it misses the real one and fires on radicals
================================================================================================================

pdfdrill's second geometric property: "a glyph whose extent encloses
another's on the same baseline is an overprint -- proved on EQ0472, and
the same book composes ⇐+⇒ into ⟺ that way. 13 rows corpus-wide carry
an unaccounted overlay (mielke 9, gilmore 2, fong-spivak 2)." Checked
on the ink of that same book before building anything on it.

IT DOES NOT WORK ON A RASTER, and it fails in both directions.

====================================================================
1. FALSE NEGATIVE -- on the case it was proved on
====================================================================

EQ0472's display line is p155 line 18 of mielke-geometrodynamics. Its
two `\Longleftrightarrow` are SINGLE connected components, 98 x 33 px,
area 703 each. Over the book's three long-arrow display lines there are
ZERO component pairs overlapping in both x and y.

TeX composes a long arrow from `arrowdblleft` + a double bar +
`arrowdblright`. On the page those pieces TOUCH, so the sweep sees one
component: there are no two boxes for one to contain. Whatever the text
layer records as an overlay, the ink has already merged.

====================================================================
2. FALSE POSITIVE -- at a rate that would drown the finding
====================================================================

Every 9th display line of the book, 128 lines, 4,082 components. Pairs
overlapping by more than half the narrower one in x AND overlapping in
y:

    100 pairs on 18 of 128 lines (14%)

Every example inspected is ordinary typesetting:

    p24  a radical, 294 x 65 px, over the eight glyphs under its bar
    p47  `\left( ... \right)`, 106 x 65, over its contents
    p47  `\stackrel{*}{\pi}`, the accent over the letter
    p86  `\left[ \begin{array}{c|c} ... \right]`, 114 x 137, over its cells

A rule keyed on containment would flag a radical, a big delimiter or a
stacked accent as an overlay on every second or third display line.

====================================================================
3. WHY THIS DOES NOT CONTRADICT PDFDRILL'S MEASUREMENT
====================================================================

Theirs runs on GLYPH RECORDS. There a radical is ONE record whose box
legitimately spans its contents, and a composed arrow is SEVERAL
records with overlapping boxes -- the two cases are distinguishable
because the text layer kept them apart. On a raster they are not:
the radical's box still contains its contents, and the arrow's pieces
are no longer separate objects.

So containment is a TEXT-LAYER property. It is worth having there, and
it is exactly the evidence a scan does not carry -- which was the
reason for looking at the ink in the first place.

====================================================================
4. WHAT THE INK CAN DO INSTEAD: width against the rendered reference
====================================================================

The composition is still visible, by size rather than by containment.
Compare a component's width with the claimed glyph rendered from the
font at the size its own height implies:

    line          reading                ink width   cmsy10 reference
    p155 (x2)     \Longleftrightarrow      98 px     short ⇔ = 56 px
    p83           \Leftrightarrow          50 px     short ⇔ = 56 px

Both readings agree with the ink, so there is no overprint error on
these lines. The test that WOULD catch one -- a short ⇔ read where the
page prints the long ⟺, or one glyph read where two are superimposed --
is this width residual, and it is the machinery 661 already built:
render the claimed glyph, measure the ink, report the disagreement.

NOT BUILT. Parked at the user's direction, 2026-09-12.

====================================================================
WHAT THIS DOES NOT SAY
====================================================================

  * One book, every 9th DISPLAY line. Inline text lines were not
    sampled, and they carry `\not`, `\stackrel` and accents in prose.
  * 12 of the 100 pairs were judged by eye, not all of them. The
    claim "every example inspected is legitimate" covers those 12.
  * The pair test was x-overlap > 50% of the narrower component AND
    y-overlap > 0. A different threshold gives a different rate; the
    false NEGATIVE in section 1 does not depend on the threshold,
    because there is only one component there.

A BY-CATCH. On p155 line 18 MathPix read the `+` of `\stackrel{+}{\Omega}`
as the CJK character 十 (U+5341) -- next to the Arabic ل (U+0644) that
661 found on two other lines of the same book.
