649 - the aligned comparison, and two predictions of mine refuted
==================================================================

out/648 counted constructs over whole documents. This matches each
MathPix expression to the author's own, on the 7 documents that have
both a source and an evidence table: 5,643 expressions.

`tools/alignlatex.py`.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
THE METHOD IS A LEAVE-ONE-OUT, WHICH IS THE POINT

Both sides are normalised and matched on the result, and normalisation
is what decides the answer -- so it is not applied as one lump. Each
MathPix habit is a separate rule, and the match rate is measured with
ALL rules on and then again with each one LEFT OUT. The rows that stop
matching when rule C is removed are exactly the rows where C was
substituted. An aligned count, obtained without ever asserting that a
particular row was changed.

`\cdots` IS IN THE LIST AS A CONTROL. out/648 measured it at 1.1x, so
it should contribute nothing, and a large share for it would mean the
ladder was measuring itself. IT SCORES EXACTLY 0 ON ALL SEVEN
DOCUMENTS.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
REFUTED PREDICTION 1: THE AUTHOR'S MACROS

I expected macro expansion to move the number a lot. 0902.0431 defines
149 `\def` macros -- `\ga` for alpha and so on -- and it is 56% of the
sample; MathPix reads the PAGE and emits the expansion, while the
author's file holds the shorthand, so comparing them unexpanded should
have been measuring the author's typing habits.

Expansion was implemented and the total moved 32.5% -> 32.1%. DOWN.

It matters locally -- `\operatorname` drops from 84 to 8, because the
rows it was explaining were matching for the wrong reason -- but it
does not explain the unmatched majority. My reason for expecting it
was sound and the effect is not there.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
REFUTED PREDICTION 2: SEGMENTATION EXPLAINS THE REST

The other candidate was that MathPix cuts expressions at different
boundaries from the author's `$...$`. Of the 3,829 unmatched:

     13.2%   appear VERBATIM inside an author expression
             -- MathPix split what the author wrote as one
     25.4%   CONTAIN a whole author expression
             -- MathPix merged what the author separated
     61.5%   neither

SEGMENTATION IS REAL AND IT IS NOT THE MAJORITY. It accounts for 38.6%
of the unmatched, and MERGING is twice SPLITTING. A row of an evidence
table is not reliably one author formula, which is worth knowing on
its own.

Full accounting of the 5,643:

    32.1%  matched, verbatim or after the seven rules
     8.9%  a sub-expression of an author expression
    17.2%  spanning several author expressions
    41.7%  unaccounted

THE 41.7% IS NOT AN ERROR RATE AND MUST NOT BE QUOTED AS ONE. It is
the residual of a SEVEN-RULE normaliser. Font commands, `\text{}`,
array environments and `\left.` are all untreated, and each would move
it. What fraction is real OCR error is unmeasured.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
THE FINDING THAT SURVIVES: COMMON AND VISIBLE ARE DIFFERENT AXES

Every aligned class was rendered both ways and the ink compared:

    class            aligned rows    renders
    braces {X}->X            764     IDENTICAL ink
    left/right               245     GEOMETRIC, +4.9%
    spacing                   29     GEOMETRIC, +14.6%
    mid                       23     GEOMETRIC, +2.7%
    operatorname               8     IDENTICAL ink
    empty group                6     GEOMETRIC, +8.9%
    cdots                      0     IDENTICAL ink

THE LARGEST CLASS PRODUCES NO VISIBLE RESIDUAL AT ALL. `a_{1}` for
`a_1` is 764 of the 1,075 aligned substitutions and renders to
identical ink, blob for blob. Together with `\operatorname` and
`\cdots` that is 772 cosmetic against 303 geometric.

So a table that reports "MathPix changed this expression" would be
72% noise from a reader's point of view, and the geometric filter is
what separates a difference from a residual.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
AND THE LIMITATION THAT BOUNDS ALL OF IT

THE LEAVE-ONE-OUT ONLY SEES ROWS THAT MATCH. A substitution severe
enough to push a row out of matching entirely is invisible to its own
column. That is why `empty group` scores 6 here while out/648 counted
7,557 prescript occurrences across 333 documents: the rows carrying
them are mostly in the 41.7%, not in the matched 32%.

The aligned counts are therefore a floor for the cosmetic classes and
a SEVERE UNDERCOUNT for the disruptive ones -- which is the opposite
of the ranking a reader would take from the table if it were quoted
without this paragraph.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
NOT CLAIMED

  * SEVEN DOCUMENTS, and 0902.0431 is 56% of the rows. The totals are
    close to that one document's behaviour.
  * Author math extraction covers `$..$`, `$$..$$`, `\[..\]` and four
    environments. Arrays, matrices and custom environments are missed
    and would appear as unmatched.
  * The geometry deltas are one minimal expression per class, measured
    in out/648, and depend on content.
  * Nothing in the pipeline was changed.
