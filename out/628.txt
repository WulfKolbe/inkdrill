2. THE PERFORMANCE GAIN, MEASURED HERE

Six real pages, 19,649 to 126,974 runs each. Best of five per
measurement, gc collected between, and THE WHOLE THING RUN TWICE WITH
THE TREES IN OPPOSITE ORDER, because a single-shot timing in a fixed
order is not a measurement.

    operation              main      branch    change
    sweep Capture.NONE    1.638 s    1.201 s   -26.7%   (1.36x)
    sweep Capture.GRAPH   1.931 s    1.874 s    -3.0%   (1.03x)
    _table_cells          4.912 s    3.897 s   -20.7%   (1.26x)

    order reversed: NONE 1.654/1.187, GRAPH 1.931/1.878,
    cells 4.876/3.888 -- the same to within a percent.

THEIR OWN CLAIM IS ACCURATE AND IS NOT OVERSTATED. out/006 says -27.5%
and -31.0% at Capture.NONE and -1.9% and -4.5% at Capture.GRAPH; I
measure -26.7% and -3.0%. out/005 even records a step that is "1.5%
SLOWER, which is why it is written this way". Nothing here needed
discounting.

WHERE IT LANDS IN THE PIPELINE. Both levels are live: `Capture.NONE`
in `nest` (the ink and background passes) and `pngio`; `Capture.GRAPH`
in `__main__`, `aggregate`, `qc` and `nest`'s third pass. The gain is
almost entirely at NONE, and the end-to-end figure that matters is
`_table_cells` at **-20.7%**, because that is what every lattice, every
`compare` and every `reportcompare` page pays.

A SINGLE-SHOT MEASUREMENT WOULD HAVE MISREPORTED THIS. My first pass
timed one run per page at Capture.GRAPH only and read 1.06x overall
with three of six pages apparently SLOWER. That is noise at a level
where the true effect is 3%, and it would have been reported as "no
gain" against a change that is worth a fifth of the lattice.
