--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE ANSWER TO THE QUESTION ASKED

DOES \mathscr READ HIGHER THAN \mathcal? YES, and by a wide margin:

    glyph    mathcal J    mathscr J
      L          1            4
      G          1            3
      J          1            5-8

The question posed 1-2 against 0-1. The measurement is 3-8 against a
FLAT 1. \mathcal (cmsy10) reads exactly one junction site on all
three letters at all three sizes; \mathscr (rsfs10) reads three to
eight and varies by letter.

DOES J ADD ANYTHING TO H? YES, and this is the stronger half.
\mathcal reads 1/0 -- one junction, NO holes -- on L, G and J alike.
On holes alone \mathcal is indistinguishable from upright L, upright
G, upright J, italic L, italic G and mathfrak L and J: all read 0.
Junctions separate \mathcal from four of those six.

The reverse also holds. mathfrak J reads 0/0 where mathcal J reads
1/0 -- identical hole counts, different junction counts.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
2. WHERE THE FAMILIES OVERLAP, WHICH IS THE FINDING

    upright L    2/0        italic L     2/0     IDENTICAL
    upright J    1/0        italic J     1/0     identical at 40, 80
    mathcal L    1/0        mathfrak L   1/0     IDENTICAL
    mathcal J    1/0        italic J     1/0     IDENTICAL at 40, 80

UPRIGHT AND ITALIC ARE NOT SEPARATED by either channel on L or J.
That is expected -- cmr10 and cmmi10 draw the same skeleton with a
slant -- and it means this feature cannot answer "was this italic",
only "which FAMILY of shapes".

MATHCAL AND MATHFRAK COLLIDE ON L at 1/0/3, exactly. A single glyph
cannot separate those two.

So the families do not partition. What separates cleanly on this set
is \mathscr and \mathbb (high J, H >= 1 on every letter) from
\mathcal, upright, italic and mathfrak (low J, H = 0 on most).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
3. STABILITY ACROSS SIZE, WHICH IS THE WEAKNESS

Sixteen of the twenty measured glyphs are IDENTICAL at all three
sizes. Four are not:

    italic G     2/0/4 -> 3/0/5     at 80px
    italic J     1/0/3 -> 3/1/3     at 160px
    italic g     1/1/1 -> 2/1/2     at 160px
    mathscr J    5/1/5 -> 8/4/3     at 160px
    mathbb G     8/4/3 -> 12/5/4    at 160px
    mathscr G    3/1/9 -> 3/1/3     at 80px (endpoints only)

italic J GAINS A HOLE between 80 and 160 px -- the hook closes
against the stem at higher resolution. A feature that changes its
HOLE count with dpi is not a stable identity, and hole count is the
channel this project has treated as topological and reliable since
218.

mathbb G goes 8 -> 12 junctions on the same glyph. The double-struck
strokes come apart under thinning differently at different sizes.

So: J and H are stable for two thirds of this set and not for the
rest, and the instability is concentrated in the families with the
most internal structure -- exactly the ones J was supposed to
distinguish.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
4. A DEFECT FOUND AND FIXED BEFORE ANY NUMBER ABOVE

The first version counted skeleton pixels with three or more
8-NEIGHBOURS. An 8-connected CORNER has two such pixels: on a 1 px
`L`, the pixel above the corner sees up, down, and the corner's
right-hand neighbour diagonally. It reported TWO junctions on a
letter that has none.

The definition is now the CROSSING NUMBER -- half the cyclic total
variation of the 8-neighbourhood -- which gives 1 at a stroke end, 2
along a stroke AND AT A CORNER, and 3+ at a branch. Verified on a
plus (1 site, 4 ends), an L (0 sites, 2 ends) and a T (1 site, 3
ends), all three in the suite so the definition cannot revert.

Had that not been caught, every serif and every corner in the table
above would have counted as a junction and the families would have
appeared to separate on stroke count rather than on branching.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
5. ON 0707.4470_FO0175

NOT CHECKABLE FROM THIS TABLE, and I would rather say so than imply
the channel is ready.

The separation that exists is between \mathscr/\mathbb and the rest.
If FO0175 turns on \mathcal against \mathscr specifically, the
numbers support it -- 1 against 3-8, no overlap on any of L, G, J.
But three letters at three sizes from ONE font per family is not a
typeface identifier: it is 20 measurements, and four of them move
with size.

What would make it one: the same table over every letter of each
font, and over the SECOND font of each family where one exists
(cmbx10 against cmr10, eufb10 against eufm10). A family whose two
weights disagree is not a family the feature can name. That is a
measurement, not a build, and it is the next one -- not this one.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
6. POPULATION

Six fonts from the TeX tree -- cmr10, cmmi10, cmsy10, rsfs10,
eufm10, msbm10 -- four glyph names, three sizes, 20 renderable
combinations of 24. Four absent: cmsy10, rsfs10 and msbm10 carry no
lowercase `g`, which is the same gap that dropped 11 characters from
`\mathscr{M a p}` in pdfdrill's 2103.01507.

Rendered by inkdrill's own chain, so nothing here depends on
ghostscript or on a PDF.
