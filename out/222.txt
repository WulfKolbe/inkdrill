222 - corpus sweep: ring labels against ring topology
========================================================================

ANSWER:

    unslashed-ring labels whose ink has holes 2 / chi -1     1
    slashed-ring labels whose ink has holes 1 / chi  0       0

The one is EQ0133 of Voloshin's Introduction to Graph and Hypergraph
Theory -- the case 218 found by hand. Sweeping 1,101 documents turned
up no second instance and no inverse instance at all.

AND THE SWEEP CORRECTS 218. See section 4: a blackboard-bold
`\mathbb{O}` is NOT a one-hole ring, so hole count alone does not
separate it from `\emptyset`. 218's separation was sound on the pair
it measured and its general statement was too strong.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. POPULATION AND THE RULE THAT BUILT IT

1,101 documents with both a `report.tex` and a `report-crops/`
directory. Measured on MathPix's OWN crop of each equation, so no
region mapping stands between a label and its ink.

A row enters the population only when a ring command is the LAST
MATHS TOKEN of its LaTeX -- because that is the one case where
POSITION SUPPLIES IDENTITY. 117 established that per-component
correspondence between a render and a scan is not recoverable by
position; the exception is an endpoint, where "the last symbol" and
"the last glyph" name the same thing.

    unslashed  \mathbb{O}, \mathrm{O}, O, \circ, \bigcirc     27 rows
    slashed    \emptyset, \varnothing, \slashed{0}            18 rows

ONE CORRECTION TO THAT RULE, MADE BEFORE THE COUNT. The first version
stripped ANY trailing `\text{...}` when deciding what the last token
was, so `\circ \text{ for all } x` read as ending in `\circ` -- final
in the LaTeX and not in the ink, leaving the rightmost component a
letter. Trailing text is now stripped only when it contains nothing
but punctuation and space. That moved 5 rows into the population and
took the consistent-unslashed count from 13 to 19.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
2. FINDING THE GLYPH: A SHAPE TEST, NOT A PUNCTUATION HEURISTIC

The measured glyph is the rightmost component that HAS AT LEAST ONE
HOLE and whose bbox aspect lies between 0.4 and 1.5. The label names
a ring, so the search is for a ring.

Two punctuation heuristics were tried first and both are recorded
because the way they failed is the point. Skip components shorter
than half the row MEDIAN: a 4x11 comma cleared that on crops full of
subscripts, and five rows were measured on the comma. Skip components
shorter than 0.35 of the TALLEST: that skipped the `\emptyset` itself
on crops carrying a tall delimiter, and two slashed rows went to
zero holes.

Each fixed one class and broke the other, and choosing between them
by which answer looked better is tuning a threshold to the result.
The shape test needs no height threshold at all: a comma has no
hole, a delimiter is not ring-shaped.

4. THE CORRECTION TO 218

Four `\mathbb{O}` rows measured 3, 4, 4 and 6 holes. They are not
errors and not contradictions -- a blackboard-bold O is DOUBLE
STRUCK, an outer ring and an inner ring joined by doubling strokes,
and those strokes enclose extra regions. Rendered from 2306.13098
EQ0005, 26 x 26 px:

    ............###...........
    ........###########.......
    ......#####....#####......
    ....#####.......######....
    ...###.##........##.###...
    ..###.##..........#..##...
    ..##..##..........##..##..
    .##...#...........##...##.
    .#...##............##...#.
    ##...##............##...##
    .#....#............##..##.
    .##...##..........##...##.
    ..##..##..........##.##...
    ...###.##........##.###...
    ....#####........#####....
    .....######....######.....
    .......############.......
    ..........######..........

Nested rings, no diagonal. So:

  218 SAID: an O is holes 1 / chi 0, a slashed zero is holes 2 /
      chi -1, and the classes differ by an integer.
  TRUE FOR a plain `O` and `\circ`: all 22 measured at holes 1.
  FALSE FOR `\mathbb{O}`: measured at 3, 4, 4 and 6, and a
      double-struck ring could in principle reach exactly 2.

Hole count alone therefore does NOT separate `\mathbb{O}` from
`\emptyset`. 218's conclusion on its own pair still holds, because
that rested on the SHAPE as well -- EQ0133's glyph is a single ring
with one diagonal, pixel-identical to EQ0124's, and nothing like the
nested rings above. But the general claim was too strong and this
sweep is what caught it: the corpus contained the counterexample and
the pair did not.

The narrower true statement: a single ring with a rim-to-rim stroke
has one more hole than the same ring without it. That is still an
identity. It just does not license reading a hole count without
knowing how many rings are in the glyph.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
5. WHAT THIS DOES NOT SAY

- NOT that MathPix substitutes ring commands once per corpus. The
  population is only rows where the ring command is FINAL -- 45 of
  many thousands of equations. A substitution mid-expression is
  invisible here, and that is most of them.
- NOT that the 22 consistent unslashed rows are right. Holes 1 is
  consistent with `O`; it is equally consistent with `0`, `D`, `o`
  and any other single-ring glyph. Consistency is not confirmation.
- NOT an error rate. One contradiction in 45 rows is a count, and 45
  rows selected by an endpoint rule are not a sample of anything.
