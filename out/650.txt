650 - the missing rules, and why raising the match rate was the wrong goal
===========================================================================

Five rules added to `tools/alignlatex.py`, chosen from the data rather
than guessed: the tokens enriched in unmatched rows were counted first.

    token          in unmatched   in matched
    \mathfrak            22.7%         0.0%
    \boldsymbol          13.9%         0.0%
    \Perp                 1.7%         0.0%
    \prime                4.6%         0.1%
    \rightarrow           9.0%         0.4%

NOT "RARE IN MATCHED" -- ZERO. No matched row contains `\mathfrak` or
`\boldsymbol` at all. Authors reach for a macro (`\R`, `\g`) or a group
(`{\bf R}`); MathPix names the font it sees. The two never agree
textually however the glyphs are set.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
THE RESULT, AND TWO MORE BLANK CONTROLS

    rule       rows it carries
    fonts             182
    arrows             54
    text                9
    primes              0
    over                0

    match rate  32.1%  ->  36.3%

`primes` and `over` contribute EXACTLY ZERO, joining `\cdots` from
out/649. Three rules in the set now fire blank, which is what keeps
the others honest: a ladder where every rule scores well is a ladder
measuring itself.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
BUT THE GAIN IS NOWHERE NEAR WHAT THE PREVALENCE PREDICTED

`\mathfrak` is in 22.7% of unmatched rows -- about 870 of them -- and
the fonts rule recovered 182. The other ~690 contain `\mathfrak` AND
something else, and a row matches only if EVERY difference in it is
normalised at once.

MEASURED, NOT ASSUMED:

    expression length     rows   matched    rate
        0-10 chars        1100       798   72.5%
       11-20              1168       551   47.2%
       21-40              1581       485   30.7%
       41-80              1237       179   14.5%
       81-160              492        34    6.9%
        161+               65         1    1.5%

    unmatched rows by how many rule classes they contain
        0 classes   511  (14.2%)
        1           701  (19.5%)
        2          1129  (31.4%)
        3           735  (20.4%)
        4           390  (10.8%)
        5+          129  ( 3.6%)
    median: 2

THE MATCH RATE IS A FUNCTION OF LENGTH, NOT OF HOW MANY RULES I WRITE.
72.5% at ten characters and 1.5% past 160. Two-thirds of unmatched
rows carry two or more classes, so each new rule has to share credit
with the rest and the marginal return collapses.

So the unmatched fraction is NOT an error rate and never was going to
become one by adding rules. Exact-string matching is the right
instrument for short expressions and the wrong one for long ones, and
out/649's plan -- "write the missing rules and watch the 41.7% fall"
-- was mine and was wrong about what it would show. It did show which
rules are classes and which are settled, which is worth having.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
THE PRODUCT IS THE 511, NOT THE 63.7%

511 rows differ from the author's source and contain NONE of the
twelve classes. Of those:

    213 (42%)  appear verbatim inside an author expression
               -- a boundary difference, not a content one
    298        remain. Median 12 characters, p90 36.

AND THE SHORT ONES ARE FRAGMENTS:

    0902.0431   FO0340    )
    0902.0431   FO1514    (
    1510.06699  FO0219    C
    2106.07890  FO0011    *
    0707.4470   FO0163    t<
    0902.0431   FO1173    f:
    0902.0431   FO1526    =0
    0902.0431   FO2434    z(
    2106.07890  FO0016    x=
    2501.06662  FO0149    b=

A lone `)` is not a formula. These are rows where MathPix cut a piece
out of an expression and emitted it as one, and they are a defect
class an evidence table should carry in its own right -- narrowed to
298 candidates out of 5,643 by twelve rules and a boundary test,
rather than left inside a 63.7% that means nothing.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
TWO INSTRUMENTS THAT SHARE NO CODE AGREE

The 260 rows of 0902.0431 measured in out/645 have an IMAGE-side
class. Crossed against the SOURCE-side verdict:

    source           SILENT   GREY   RED
    matched              44     72     5
    known class          80     36    12
    boundary              3      3     1
    RESIDUE               0      4     0

ALL FOUR RESIDUE ROWS ARE GREY. Unaccounted in the author's source and
unplaceable in the ink, decided by two computations with nothing in
common -- one a string comparison against a 2009 e-print, the other a
blob-profile fit against a 400 dpi raster.

FOUR ROWS IS FOUR ROWS. The direction is right and the number is too
small to carry weight; the residue is concentrated later in the table
than the measured 260 reach.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
NOT CLAIMED

  * SEVEN DOCUMENTS, 0902.0431 is 56% of the rows.
  * The fonts rule ERASES the font, so `\mathfrak{g}` and `g` become
    one string. That can create false matches; the leave-one-out
    column says it is carrying 182 rows and a reader can discount it.
  * The 298 were not individually inspected beyond the sample above.
  * The cross-check is n=4.
  * Nothing in the pipeline was changed.
