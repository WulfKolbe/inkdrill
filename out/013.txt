STEP 4 -- the flush fixtures
============================

ALL SEVEN PASS. No behaviour was found wrong: `sweep` is
field-for-field identical to `_sweep_reference` on every one, at both
axes, both connectivities and all three capture levels. The
retire/flush machinery handles every case the review named.

The seven, added to FIXTURES
----------------------------
  leading_blank_lines                  blank lines before any ink
  trailing_blank_lines                 blank lines after the last ink
  two_blank_lines_between_components   two blanks between two
                                       multi-line components
  full_width_line_between_regions      a full-width run alone on its
                                       line, between two ink regions
  open_at_eof                          a component whose last line is
                                       the mask's last line
  single_column                        width 1, five lines tall
  single_row                           height 1, six columns wide

Coverage
--------
The fixture set is 23 masks (16 before), and `test_fixtures` now runs

    23 masks x 2 axes x 2 conns x 3 captures = 276 checks

each check comparing nodes, components AND events field by field, plus
the G2 cycle-rank identity. The count is asserted rather than left as
`> 0`, so a fixture deleted or a capture level dropped from the product
fails here instead of quietly shrinking the run.

Note that `single_column` and `single_row` swap roles under the axis
loop, so the height-1 mask is also swept as a width-1 mask and back.

The interesting result is what the fixtures could NOT catch
-----------------------------------------------------------
Every one of these fixtures is checked by comparing two
implementations, and BOTH carry their own copy of the final flush.
A flush lost from both is invisible to the whole gate.

Demonstrated: a throwaway copy of the package with the final-closure
block disabled in `sweep` AND `_sweep_reference`

    tests.test_sweep_equiv: Ran 15 tests, FAILED (failures=1)
    FAIL: test_every_component_closes_exactly_once

Fourteen of the fifteen tests pass with every CLOSE-at-EOF event
missing -- the 276 field-by-field comparisons included, because both
sides lost the same events, and G2 included, because CLOSE is an event
and not a count. That is exactly the silent loss the review predicted.

So step 4 also adds `test_every_component_closes_exactly_once`: over
the whole fixture set at both event-bearing capture levels, the number
of CLOSE events must equal the component count and no component may
close twice. Every component closes exactly once -- at a blank line by
G7, or at EOF -- so this is an invariant of the sweep rather than a
comparison, and it is the assertion a lost flush actually trips. It
was verified to hold on a glyph page and on random masks at densities
0.01-0.8 before it was asserted.

Not claimed
-----------
  * The fixtures are hand-written masks of at most 7 lines. They say
    the cases are handled, not that they are handled at scale.
  * `test_every_component_closes_exactly_once` holds the CLOSE count to
    the component count. It does not check that each CLOSE is on the
    right LINE; a flush that fired at the wrong line would still pass it
    and would still be caught by the oracle comparison, which is the
    other half of the pair.
