681 - nest's forest checked by a third rule: containment, not a neighbour lookup
===============================================================================

out/680 closed with nest unchecked, and said why: `nest` finds a
region's parent by ONE LOOKUP -- the pixel directly above its
topmost-leftmost -- and `tests/_hole_attribution.py` credits a hole to
the pixel directly LEFT of its first pixel in raster order. Same family:
a neighbour of an extreme pixel in raster order. Neither can refute the
other.

====================================================================
1. THE RULE
====================================================================

`tests/_containment.py` decides parenthood by ENCLOSURE, uniformly for
ink and background:

    enclosed(R)   flood the COMPLEMENT of R inward from the ring around
                  R's bounding box; whatever the flood cannot reach is
                  enclosed by R. Connectivity stays paired: the flood
                  over the complement of an 8-connected ink region runs
                  at 4, over a 4-connected background region at 8.
    parent(X)     the region with the SMALLEST enclosed set among those
                  enclosing all of X; None when none does.

Smallest-enclosing is "innermost", and it is unique: if two regions both
enclose X, one encloses the other and their enclosed sets are strictly
nested. No raster order, no adjacency, no cycle rank, no union-find, no
run index. Keys are `nest`'s own Region fields -- (kind, bbox, area) --
so neither side has to expose a pixel.

The third row is the point. That oracle's own docstring says it shares
nest's parent rule and must not be used to check nest; the mutant proves
the warning is not theoretical. `nest.py` was restored unchanged.

====================================================================
3. A FINDING ABOUT THE CORPUS, NOT THE CODE
====================================================================

NO SMALL REAL NESTED STRUCTURE EXISTS on the pages searched. Ink at
depth >= 2 occurs only inside page-wide frames and ruled grids --
1,779 px on kohlhase-omdoc p49, 1,822 px on cardona p181 -- and a window
cut out of one is not enclosed, because the rule crosses the window edge
and the interior stops being a hole. A pixel oracle over 3 Mpx costs
hundreds of MB in Python sets, so the full frames were not run.

Hence the framed windows: the glyphs, their counters and their positions
are the page's; the enclosing rectangle is drawn. That is stated in the
test rather than hidden, and it is the only semi-synthetic input here.

`tests/test_nest_containment_corpus.py` keeps the campaign repeatable,
opt-in on `INKDRILL_CORPUS`, like the other corpus modules.

    suite   1,306 -> 1,316 (8 hermetic + 2 corpus-gated), 40 skipped

====================================================================
WHAT THIS DOES NOT SAY
====================================================================

  * The oracle is O(bbox) in Python per region, so it checks WINDOWS,
    not pages. A whole 3400x4677 page has never been through it.
  * It checks the forest -- parents, kinds, extents -- not `nest`'s
    performance claims, and not `InkPass.complete()`.
  * Page-wide frames and ruled grids, the structures where depth >= 2
    really occurs in this corpus, remain unchecked by this rule at
    their true size.
  * 4-connected foreground is still untested anywhere, because the
    package never uses it.
