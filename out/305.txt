2. THREE POPULATIONS, AND WHY THE FIRST TWO WERE WRONG

POPULATION A -- page-wide rows, 15 pages, 723 rows, 25,835 gaps:

    p50 1.00   p90 13.33   p95 26.00   p99 35.00   max 389
    >= 3x median: 95.0% of rows      >= 10x: 82.3%

Wrong, and diagnosably so. `mathstruct.rows` groups by vertical
overlap across the WHOLE PAGE WIDTH, so one "row" spans an equation
and its equation number with 400 px of margin between them. That gap
is page layout, not a typeset space.

POPULATION B -- gaps within a lines.json region, 15 pages, 5,143
rows, 75,952 gaps:

    p50 1.00   p90 6.33   p95 22.00   p99 33.25
    >= 3x median: 36.2%              >= 10x: 24.5%

Also wrong. lines.json NESTS table regions, so the same cell's ink is
counted as `table`, `table_column`, `table_row` and `simple_cell` --
4,816 of the 5,143 rows are that nesting, against 202 real text and
math lines.

POPULATION C -- `math` and `text` regions only, 20 pages, 202 rows,
7,894 gaps. The restriction is decided by 306's PURPOSE -- where a
`\quad` could go inside a maths string -- not by which population
gives a break. This is the last cut for that reason; narrowing until
a knee appears would be choosing the population from the answer.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
4. WHY THE MEASURE FAILS, WHICH IS NOT THE SAME AS THE IDEA FAILING

A typeset space in maths IS wider than an inter-glyph gap -- the
premise is sound. What fails is the NORMALISER. The row median is
1.00 px on half the rows measured, because most adjacent glyph pairs
in set mathematics touch or nearly touch. Dividing by a median of 1
turns every ordinary 6 px gap into a 6x ratio, so the statistic
saturates: 87.6% of rows have "a gap several times the median"
because the median is not a gap, it is a kerning artefact.

That is a property of the divisor, not of the page. A measure with a
denominator that does not collapse -- the row's x-height, its median
GLYPH WIDTH, or the mode of the positive gaps rather than their
median -- might separate where this one cannot. I have not measured
any of them and I am not going to assert that one would.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
5. POPULATION

One book, 1511.08771, 11,232 pages, of which 20 sampled at fixed
intervals (20, 40, ... 380) plus page 108. Gaps measured between
consecutive components in a row, clamped at zero for overlapping
boxes -- a kerned pair is a real zero-width gap and dropping it would
raise the median. Rows under 4 glyphs excluded.

One book is one book. A different typeface or a tighter setting would
move these numbers, and nothing here claims otherwise.
