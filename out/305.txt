4. WHY THE MEASURE FAILS -- SUPERSEDED, SEE out/309.txt

    THE EXPLANATION BELOW IS WRONG. It says the row median gap is
    1.00 px on half the rows. 309 measured it: ZERO of 202 rows have
    a median gap at or below 1 px; the median gap is 5.0 px at p50.
    I mistook the RATIO's p50 of 1.00 -- which is 1.00 by
    construction, since half of any row's gaps lie below that row's
    median -- for the denominator being 1, and built a mechanism on a
    tautology.

    The measure does fail, and section 3's numbers stand. 309
    re-normalises by median GLYPH WIDTH on the same rows and finds a
    bimodal distribution with a trough at 0.45 glyph widths, so the
    signal is real and this normaliser was simply the wrong one.

4. (superseded) WHY THE MEASURE FAILS

A typeset space in maths IS wider than an inter-glyph gap -- the
premise is sound. What fails is the NORMALISER. The row median is
1.00 px on half the rows measured, because most adjacent glyph pairs
in set mathematics touch or nearly touch. Dividing by a median of 1
turns every ordinary 6 px gap into a 6x ratio, so the statistic
saturates: 87.6% of rows have "a gap several times the median"
because the median is not a gap, it is a kerning artefact.

That is a property of the divisor, not of the page. A measure with a
denominator that does not collapse -- the row's x-height, its median
GLYPH WIDTH, or the mode of the positive gaps rather than their
median -- might separate where this one cannot. I have not measured
any of them and I am not going to assert that one would.

AND IT SHOWS WHERE SECTION 4 WENT WRONG.

309 recorded that section 4's explanation is false, measuring 0 of
202 rows with a median gap <= 1 px. That correction is right, and the
re-run says WHY the claim was made.

Measured directly, raw pixels, no normalisation:

    rows measured                    983   (page-wide grouping)
    median of the row median gaps    5.0 px
    range                            2.0 to 382.0 px
    rows whose median gap <= 1 px      0 of 983

So the median gap is 5 px, not 1 px, and no row is near 1 px.

The "1.00" in section 4 is THIS FILE'S OWN p50, one screen above it —
and that p50 is a RATIO, gap divided by the row's own median gap. It
is 1.00 because dividing a distribution by its own median puts the
median at 1.00. It is not a length, it cannot be a length, and it is
1.00 on every population that will ever be measured this way.

Section 4 read its own normalised statistic as a pixel value and then
offered it as the physical explanation for the saturation it had just
found. The saturation is real and the explanation was circular: the
number cited as the cause is an artefact of the normalisation that
produced the effect.

The original text above is left as written. This section corrects it;
309 found it first.
