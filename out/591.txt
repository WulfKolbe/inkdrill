591 - the ring channel against the speckle floor, with a dpi on it
====================================================================

THE FINDING IS SAFE, AND NOW HAS A NUMBER.

    crop        size      comp   holes   holes/component
    EQ0124    531 x 104     38       9        0.237
    EQ0133    873 x  53     44       7        0.159

    speckle regime, from 589                              ~3.4

Both sit an order of magnitude below the floor -- 14x and 21x clear.
Neither is near 3. The comparison does not need re-taking, and the
letter can go out.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. AND THE WHOLE SWEEP POPULATION IS BELOW IT, NOT JUST THESE TWO

Two crops being clean would still leave the question open for the
other 1,101 documents, so the ratio was taken across the population
the sweep ran on: 400 crops drawn with a stated seed from 93,028 EQ
crops in 856 documents, measured the same way `ringmeasure.py` does
(magick to PNG, threshold 200, `ink_only`) because a second decode
path would answer a different question.

    holes per component     p50 0.225   p90 0.488   p99 0.890
                            max 1.636

    at or above 1.0 h/c       2 of 400    0.50%
    at or above 2.0 h/c       0 of 400    0.00%
    at or above 3.0 h/c       0 of 400    0.00%

NOT ONE CROP IN 400 REACHES EVEN 2.0, and the maximum observed is
less than half the speckle level. EQ0124 at 0.237 and EQ0133 at 0.159
sit at and below the population median -- they are typical crops, not
lucky ones, which is the part that makes this strong rather than
merely reassuring.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE SWEEP'S DPI: 250, NOT 300 AND NOT 600

NEITHER HARNESS RECORDED ONE. `ringscan.py` and `ringmeasure.py`
contain no dpi, no density and no resolution, and out/222.txt never
states one. That is the gap this task names, and it was real: the
number had to be derived rather than read.

The crops carry no usable density of their own --

    EQ0124   XResolution 1, ResolutionUnit None
    EQ0133   XResolution 1, ResolutionUnit None

-- so it comes from the page geometry instead: MathPix's page raster
in pixels against the PDF's MediaBox in points.

    document                    page px      page pts    dpi
    Voloshin hypergraph       1750 x 2624    504 x 756   250.0
    0707.4470                 2125 x 2750    612 x 792   250.0
    1205.3463v2               2066 x 2924    595 x 842   250.0
    2005.05886                2125 x 2750    612 x 792   250.0

Exactly 250.0 on four of four comparable documents. (A fifth,
2106.07890, reads 128.5 because pdfinfo reports its FIRST page at
1191 x 842 pt while the region is on a differently sized page -- a
per-page MediaBox, not a different render.)

So the sweep ran at 250 dpi. Above the 150 dpi where 589 found the
speckle regime, below the 300 the channel was assumed to be at, and
nowhere near 600.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
3. WHAT "EXACTLY ONE IN 1,101" IS NOW WORTH

The task's reasoning is right and the measurement supports it. A sweep
run below the speckle floor would find contradictions everywhere or
nowhere, because at 3.4 holes per component the hole count is noise
and a rule keyed on it fires arbitrarily. The observed p99 of 0.890
says the channel was nowhere near that: a crop's hole count at 250 dpi
is a property of the glyphs, not of the scan.

That upgrades "exactly one" from weak evidence to strong. It was clean
because the channel was clean, not because the sweep was blind.

ONE THING IT DOES NOT UPGRADE. 222 already corrected 218 on the shape
question -- a blackboard-bold \mathbb{O} is not a one-hole ring, so
hole count alone does not separate it from \emptyset. That correction
stands and is independent of resolution; nothing here revives 218's
general statement, only its specific pair.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
4. WHAT THIS CHANGES ABOUT QUOTING THE CHANNEL

Every hole-count figure taken from a MathPix crop is a figure at
250 dpi, and 589 shows the channel degrades below that. Two
consequences worth carrying:

  * A CDN crop's dpi is not a property of the file -- it carries none
    -- but of MathPix's render, and it is derivable from the page
    geometry as above. Quote it.
  * A comparison against ink rendered LOCALLY is a comparison across
    resolutions unless the local render is matched to 250. 498's route
    renders at 400 by default; that is a different sampling of the same
    glyph and its hole counts are not interchangeable with a crop's.

RE-RUN

    python3 tools/ringfloor.py --n 400 --seed 591

`--n`, `--seed` and `--threshold` are the split rule. The population
line prints the crop and document counts on every run.
