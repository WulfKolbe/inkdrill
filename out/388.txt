388 — pgmraw, and the streaming form
====================================

PART 1 — png16m against pgmraw, same page, four stages
------------------------------------------------------

Page 2 of the DTZ report, A3 at 600 dpi = 9921x7016 = 69.6 Mpx.

  stage              png16m      pgmraw     speedup
  rasterise            1.32 s      0.31 s      4.3x
  decode + mask       41.23 s      0.13 s    327.9x
  lattice              1.26 s      1.23 s      1.0x
  five-tuple           0.12 s      0.12 s      1.0x
  TOTAL               43.94 s      1.78 s     24.7x

  FIVE-TUPLES IDENTICAL: yes, 16 of 16 cells.

Only the decode moves, which is what 380 predicted and what pnmio's own
header has said since it was written (847x on the decode, 44x end to end).
gs is also 4.3x faster writing pgmraw than png16m — it is not compressing.

THE FILE SIZE, which is the cost of all this
--------------------------------------------

  png16m    0.4 MB     pgmraw   66.4 MB      166x larger

That is the trade in one line: PNG is small on disk and expensive to read;
PGM is large and free to read. It only pays if the bytes never land — and
they should not, because gs writes pgmraw to stdout in raster order.

PART 2 — the streaming form
---------------------------

`read_pnm_stream` is a stream IN NAME ONLY. It calls `_bytes_of(src)` and
walks a buffer holding every page: three A3 pages at 600 dpi arrive as one
199 MB object, and a 34-page render would be 2.2 GB. One page alone costs
the 66 MB raster plus the two copies `binarize` makes of it — `bytes(gray)`
and then `.translate`.

Added `stream_masks(fh, dpi, threshold)`: reads the header a byte at a time
(a read-ahead would swallow raster, and a pipe cannot give it back), then
consumes width bytes at a time and thresholds each row through the SAME
cached LUT `binarize` uses, so the rule cannot drift. The grey page never
exists as an object.

  3 pages, gs piped straight in, A3 at 600 dpi:

  form                        peak RSS     rate
  buffered (read_pnm_stream)    543 MB    41.4 pages/min
  streamed (stream_masks)       347 MB    43.2 pages/min
  streamed, mask only           215 MB   294.4 pages/min

  MASKS BYTE-IDENTICAL: yes — 3 of 3 pages, compared whole, ink counts
  1353882 / 1380108 / 1658174 both ways. Identical at every threshold
  tested (1, 64, 128, 200, 255).

So the five-tuples are byte-identical between a page held in memory and a
page streamed. Nothing added since the original design breaks on the STREAM.
Three things break on not keeping the page, and they are the finding.

WHAT NEEDS THE WHOLE PAGE — the audit you asked for
---------------------------------------------------

THE SWEEP DOES NOT. `sweep` iterates `for line, runs in _lines(mask, axis)`
and carries `open_roots` across lines — runs per line, only open components
held. That is the line-scan design and it is intact. Its INTERFACE takes a
materialised `InkMask` rather than an iterator of rows, but nothing in its
body needs one.

One correction: there is no `closed_at` in this codebase. Zero occurrences.
The mechanism is `open_roots` plus the union-find; either it was renamed or
the name is from the design and never landed. Worth knowing before building
on it.

`nest` NEEDS TWO PASSES. nest.py:251-252 sweeps the page twice —
`sweep(padded, conn=8)` for ink and `sweep(padded.inverted(), conn=4)` for
holes. A pipe cannot be rewound, so either the mask is retained or the page
is rendered twice. It also `_pad`s, which copies.

`_cell_crop` NEEDS TRUE RANDOM ACCESS, and this one is not incidental. It
indexes `mask.data[src:src+w]` at arbitrary offsets to cut each cell out,
and it can only run AFTER the lattice is known — which is only known once
the whole page has been seen. You cannot know where the cells are until the
page is over, and then you must go back into it. For the table comparison
that is fundamental, not an accident of implementation.

WHAT THIS MEANS

The table path can stream its INPUT but not discard its PAGE. The honest
form is: stream pgmraw from gs, threshold per line, keep the mask and
nothing else — 215 MB and 294 pages/min for mask construction, against 543
MB for the buffered route. The 66 MB raster and `binarize`'s two copies all
go; the 70 MB mask stays because nest re-reads it and the cell crops need it.

A path that kept NOTHING would fit the line-scan design and would not fit
this measurement: it would have to emit five-tuples for cells whose
boundaries are not yet known. That is a different instrument — one that
finds the lattice in a first pass and crops in a second — and it would read
the page twice rather than hold it once.

NOT CHANGED HERE

reportcompare and reportpages still render png16m. Switching them is the
24.7x, and it moves nothing in the output — but 386 located a defect in
reportcompare's sliver filter that also moves row indices, and both belong
in one re-measurement rather than two. The reader is in place and tested;
the callers are the next step.
