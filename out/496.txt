496 - the resolution caveat at working dpi, on the corpus
====================================================================

THE ANSWER: A FINDING, NOT A FOOTNOTE -- BUT NOT WHERE 495 POINTED.

  16.6% of compared rows change their finding class between 300 and
  600 dpi. Not near zero, so every distance in the corpus carries it.

  But it is concentrated. The strongest class survives almost intact
  and the weakest boundary carries nearly all of the movement, so the
  caveat attaches to the clean/noise cut rather than to the findings
  that come out of this project.

  And the cause is not resolution. It is that the LaTeX column is
  rendered with anti-aliasing OFF, which is a setting rather than a
  fact about ink. Turning it on takes the rendered column's hole
  instability from 75.0% to 9.4% on the same cells.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHICH CHANNEL MOVES

Per compared cell, 300 dpi against 600, over 2,636 ink-carrying rows:

    channel        rendered col     scan col      either
    components        2.8%            0.3%         3.1%
    HOLES            35.7%            0.8%        36.2%
    stacked          14.9%           11.2%        21.9%
    centred          11.9%            8.1%        16.6%
    offset           13.7%           12.0%        20.7%
    all ten                                       52.8%   (A=B says NO)

Components is the scale-invariant channel and behaves like one: 2.8%.
Holes is forty-seven times worse on the rendered side (940 cells
against 20). The three geometric channels sit between, and they move on BOTH columns, because
they are pixel-geometry predicates rather than topology.

THE TWO COLUMNS ARE NOT THE SAME KIND OF MEASUREMENT, and reading the
36.2% without that is the whole trap here. A 0.8% against a 35.7% is
the shape this project has been caught by before -- a class that
cannot fire -- so it was checked rather than quoted:

    grey levels inside the cell, at 300 dpi
      rendered column (LaTeX)      2
      scan column (photograph)   256

The rendered column is a HARD BILEVEL raster. Ghostscript's pgmraw
device with no alpha bits makes an in/out decision per pixel at the
sampling grid, so doubling the grid re-decides every near-tangent
contact and strokes touch or part. The scan column is a real greyscale
image thresholded at 200: the threshold crossing lies on a contour in
a smooth field, and sampling that field more finely does not move the
contour. That is why one column moves and the other does not, and it
is a statement about rasterisation, not about scans being better.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
2. HOW FAR THE HOLE COUNT MOVES

5,272 cell measurements (two columns x 2,636 rows):

    unchanged                4,312    81.8%
    changed                    960    18.2%
      by 1                     379     7.2%
      by 2                     224     4.2%
      by 3                     134     2.5%
      by 4                     101     1.9%
      by more                  122     2.3%

    relative change, of cells having holes at 300 dpi:
      p50 0.000    p95 0.429

So the median cell does not move at all and the 95th percentile moves
by 43% of its hole count. This is not a rounding difference at the
margin: a third of the changes are by 3 or more.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
3. DOES THE CLASS CHANGE -- THE NUMBER THE TASK ASKED FOR

    SAME class at 600 dpi     2,198    83.4%
    CHANGED class               438    16.6%

    per document: p50 16.7%, min 0.0%, max 40.0% over 37 documents,
    so no single document is carrying the rate.

BY CLASS, which is where the answer stops being one number:

    class at 300     n     keeps its class    arrivals
    absent          228      228   100.0%           0
    stable           35       35   100.0%           0
    COMPONENT       176      166    94.3%           2
    noise         1,406    1,167    83.0%         186
    clean           768      637    82.9%         177
    weak            251      193    76.9%          73

    rows whose COMPONENT verdict flips either way:  12 of 2,636

TWO OF THOSE 100% FIGURES ARE TAUTOLOGIES AND MUST NOT BE READ AS
EVIDENCE. `absent` is all-zero on both sides, and `stable` is DEFINED
as "the five-tuple did not change between 300 and 600" -- so a stable
row uses identical inputs at both resolutions and cannot be
reclassified. It is the vocabulary's own scale test, and asking
whether it survives a scale change is asking whether it equals itself.
`weak` is the complement (scale_stable false), and its 76.9% is real.

THE TRANSITIONS ARE ALMOST ALL ONE BOUNDARY:

    noise -> clean    173      clean -> noise    128
    noise -> weak      64      weak  -> noise     55
                                              --------
    those four                                    420  of 438

96% of the movement is across the distance floor, between rows scoring
0, rows scoring 1 to 7, and rows scoring above 7. That is the boundary
between "no difference" and "a difference too small to be a finding" --
the two verdicts that are least load-bearing. The component channel,
which the vocabulary calls the strongest class and the one a consumer
should act on, flips on 12 rows in 2,636.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE CAUSE, AND IT IS FIXABLE

Same document, same pages, same cells, same threshold, rendered twice:

    render         column   cells   holes move 300->600   grey levels
    aliased        left       32       24     75.0%            2
    aliased        right      33        0      0.0%          256
    ANTIALIASED    left       32        3      9.4%           20
    antialiased    right      33        0      0.0%          256

An eightfold reduction from one pair of ghostscript flags
(-dTextAlphaBits=4 -dGraphicsAlphaBits=4). The relationship is
monotone in the grey levels the device wrote: 2 levels gives 75%, 20
gives 9.4%, 256 gives 0%. Anti-aliasing puts a value between 0 and 255
on exactly the pixels whose in/out decision the grid would otherwise
decide, and thresholding that at 200 is then a contour crossing rather
than a coin flip.

THIS IS NOT A FREE CHANGE AND IS NOT MADE HERE. An anti-aliased render
thresholded at 200 has different absolute five-tuples, so it moves
every recorded distance in the corpus, not only the unstable ones.
Whether it moves them toward the scan column -- which is what would
make the comparison better rather than merely more repeatable -- is a
separate measurement that has not been taken. Recorded as the next
thing to measure, not acted on.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
5. WHAT THIS DOES TO THE THINGS HOLES CARRY

495 ended by saying hole count carries 218, 91 and every ink
comparison, and that 12% instability is therefore corpus-wide. At
working dpi it is 36.2% per row and 18.2% per cell, so the caveat is
larger than 495 estimated, not smaller. But its shape is different:

  218 (slashed zero against O) SURVIVES, as 495 already said, and for
  the same reason: a ring and a slashed ring differ by a closure that
  is nowhere near a sampling threshold.

  91 (table selection by hole count) is UNAFFECTED in kind, because it
  was changed to select by bounding box. Had it stayed on hole count,
  a 36% per-row movement would have been a serious problem for it --
  which is a second argument for that change beyond the one that
  motivated it.

  The general form "hole count is stable because it is topological"
  remains WRONG, and now measurably so on real pages rather than on
  rendered glyphs. The topology of the outline is stable; the topology
  of a bilevel sampling of that outline is not.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
6. POPULATION, SPLIT RULE, AND WHAT THE SAMPLE IS NOT

POPULATION: the 544 library directories holding both a report.pdf and
a report.compare.tsv -- exactly the documents the corpus figures are
computed over, out of 2,813 directories.

SPLIT RULE: `random.Random(496).shuffle` of that list, the first 40
taken, every display page of each. Both the seed and the count are
harness arguments. 37 of the 40 yielded rows; 3 had no scan column in
their report.tex. 295 pages, 0 refused, 2,864 rows.

ROW FILTERS, all inherited from the compare harness rather than
invented here: row 0 is the header, a row under 40 px at 300 dpi is a
sliver, a page whose lattice covers under half its table region is
refused, a lattice with under two columns is refused.

THE INSTRUMENT WAS CHECKED AGAINST THE RECORDED CORPUS BEFORE IT WAS
BELIEVED. On 1709.07941 the re-measured 300 dpi five-tuples are
IDENTICAL to the recorded ones on all 33 shared rows, and the 4 extra
rows are exactly the all-zero page-break footers `reportcompare`
drops, one per page.

REPRESENTATIVENESS, and it is imperfect:

    re-measured here, ink-carrying rows        52.8% A=B NO
    re-measured here, all rows                 48.6%
    recorded, the same 37 documents            56.8%
    recorded, all 433 documents                58.6%

The sample reads below the corpus. The reason is not sampling:
32 OF THE 37 SAMPLED DOCUMENTS HAVE A report.compare.tsv OLDER THAN
THEIR report.pdf. The recorded 56.8% describes a previous build of
those reports and this measurement describes the current one, so the
two are not comparable and the corpus-wide 58.6% is itself largely
stale. Stated rather than reconciled: reconciling two populations by
argument is what 325 was told not to do.

WHAT THIS SAMPLE CANNOT SEE. It is 37 arXiv-shaped LaTeX documents.
The Infineon handbook is not in it, and a Word document's rendered
column is not built the same way. Section 4's mechanism predicts the
same behaviour for any bilevel render, but that is a prediction.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
7. RE-RUN

    python3 tools/dpiclass.py --docs 40 --seed 496 -o rows.tsv
    python3 tools/dpisummary.py rows.tsv
    python3 tools/dpialias.py 1709.07941

`--docs` and `--seed` are the split rule. `dpiclass` renders each
display page at 300 and 600 with pgmraw, builds the compare harness's
own lattice at each, and emits both columns' five-tuples at both
resolutions; the rasters are deleted per page, so the run costs
minutes and no disk. `dpisummary` recomputes `findings.flag_of` from
each resolution and reports the transitions. `dpialias` is section 4.
