627 - testing the join across the corpus, and what it costs
====================================================================

REPO STATE. inkdrill unchanged since f3fc189. pdfdrill has moved --
634, 637, 640 -- and every report was rebuilt on 5 September.

THE HEADLINE. The join is **128x faster** than the probe it replaces
on the page-discovery step, and testing it broadly found **three
documents where it was silently under-counting**, now fixed. The
correctness fix matters more than the speed.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE PERFORMANCE, MEASURED BOTH ROUTES IN ONE RUN

`tools/joinbench.py` times, per document, the two ways of answering
"which pages carry table rows, and which rows are on them":

  PROBE  `scan_columns` renders EVERY page at 150 dpi and builds a
         lattice on each; `group_tables` groups them; an ordinal picks
         the table. This is what `reportcompare` does.
  JOIN   `rowjoin.join` reads the text layer once and the manifest.

    document                pp | probe s  join s      x | rows  found
    0049                     4 |    1.44    0.02   91.1x |    34    34
    anglemaps_SIGGRAPH_2015 12 |    5.71    0.03  176.4x |   160   160
    AttachBBlocaldim        30 |   13.43    0.09  152.4x |   584   584
    fulltext                60 |   34.05    0.28  123.2x |  2431  2431
    dmoi4                  125 |   59.52    0.43  139.3x |  4032  4032
    Geometric_topology     300 |  133.49    1.10  121.2x |  6717  6717

    totals            531 pp |  247.63s   1.94s  127.6x | 13,958 13,958

Both routes scale linearly in pages, so the ratio is roughly flat at
120-180x rather than growing. Four minutes becomes two seconds over
531 pages; the corpus is 1,331 reports.

**AND IT ANSWERS A DIFFERENT QUESTION.** The probe finds pages whose
lattice has N columns, which 595 measured as the wrong question: a run
can hold two tables and a table can span several runs. So this is not
a faster implementation of the same computation -- it is a cheaper one
that is also correct, and the speed is the smaller half of that.

WHAT IS NOT INCLUDED. The probe's result is cached by
`reportcompare`, so a repeated run pays this once rather than every
time. The 128x is the cost of the FIRST pass over a document, which
is what a rebuild pays.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
2. TESTING BROADLY FOUND A SILENT UNDER-COUNT

Running the join over documents of every size, rather than the two it
was built on, turned up a third wrap shape it could not read.

    anglemaps_SIGGRAPH_2015    16 of 160 rows      -> 160 of 160
    fulltext                 2381 of 2431 rows     -> 2431 of 2431
    dmoi4                    4026 of 4032 rows     -> 4032 of 4032

THE CAUSE. 597 handled an identifier wrapping AFTER the bibkey
(`Geometric_topology_` / `EQ0145`). This one wraps INSIDE it:

    anglemaps_SIGGRAPH_
    2015_EQ0018

The un-wrap rule joined a line ending with the COMPLETE bibkey, so it
saw nothing to join and the whole document read as 22 and 122 rows
missing across two tables. Exactly the failure class HANDOVER records
-- a guessed pattern reporting a whole class as absent -- one rule
further down than the guess it replaced.

THE FIX, and two wrong turns on the way to it:

  Deciding membership on the SQUEEZED page (all whitespace removed) is
  break-point independent and correct. But used as the PRIMARY pass it
  breaks two other things: it loses page order, and a regex over
  squeezed text invents tokens -- `0049_EQ0001` beside
  `0049_DIA_0001` squeezes to a run in which `0049_EQ00010049`
  matches the identifier shape and is then reported as an unknown
  token.

  So the squeeze became a SECOND pass, for what the first missed. But
  probing `identifier in flat` for every unfound identifier is
  O(identifiers x pages) and took the 300-page report past two
  minutes -- a correctness fix that made the thing it fixed unusable.

  The second pass now SCANS rather than probes, with one alternation
  compiled from the manifest's own strings, longest first. O(page),
  exact, and it cannot invent a token because it only matches strings
  the manifest already lists. Geometric_topology's join is 1.10 s.

Both wrong turns are in the module's G3 as the reason it is written
that way.

3. TWO CRASHES IN MY OWN TOOLS, FOUND BY RUNNING THEM WIDE

`tools/rulediff.py` raised a TypeError on **21 of 22** documents. Not
a finding about the corpus -- a tool that crashes where it should
refuse. Both causes are now named refusals:

  * `nearest()` returns None for a page with no rules, and the caller
    subtracted it. Now the page is reported.
  * a row may carry NO PAGE at all -- 488 established that inline
    formulas do -- and `max()` over a generator holding a None raises
    while comparing. Now counted and printed: two documents carry
    4,359 and 4,393 page-less rows, about 91% of their manifests.

`tools/manifestcheck.py` had the second bug too. Tracebacks across the
22: **21 -> 0.**

--------------------------------------------------------------------
4. WHAT THE WIDE RUN SAYS ABOUT THE CORPUS

21 of 22 manifests are stale, and systematically so:

    0049                 manifest and pdf written the same second
    the other 21         manifest written FIRST, pdf 9 s to 32 min later

That ordering is the two-pass `\zsavepos` compile: the manifest is
emitted from the .aux after one pass and the PDF is rewritten by the
next. The sha guard refuses all 21, correctly.

AND THEY ARE NOT COSMETICALLY STALE. The manifests describe far larger
builds than the PDFs beside them:

    manifest names page ... 15   247    64   458   296   280   220
    the pdf has ...          3    11     3    15     8     7     3

These reports are corrections and summary builds; the manifests are
from full listings. No delta computed against them would mean
anything, which is what `rulediff` now says instead of crashing.

    0049 is the one clean pair, and it is clean: sha MATCH, 175 cells,
    all four edges median 0, 100% within 1 px, and rulediff at
    +-0.001 bp. All three of 608's defects and 610's centreline are
    fixed there.
