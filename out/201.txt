201 - the component split: bracket versus rule crossing
========================================================================

ANSWER, on the two equations 196 and 200 measured:

    equation        vertical    horizontal   brackets wrongly split
    EQ0192            3 / 3        3 / 3          0 / 6
    EQ1059 (yellow)   1 / 1        1 / 1          0 / 2

Every fused crossing splits into its constituent rules. No delimiter
is split. `ink.rules[]` reports 0 on both, unchanged -- the split is a
new function, not a change to `is_rule`.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE DISCRIMINATOR, AS MEASURED IN 200

Both a crossing and a bracket are tall components with a full-height
vertical band, so the vertical band alone cannot separate them. The
horizontal bands do:

    delimiter   full-width bands at BOTH ENDS of the stem
                measured normalised positions 0.005 and 0.985
    crossing    ONE band, interior
                measured 0.50 (the three 2x2 arrays) and 0.743 (4x4)

`end = 0.1` sits in a gap two orders of magnitude wide. It is a
separation, not a tuned constant.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
2. TWO MORE GUARDS WERE NEEDED, AND BOTH CAME FROM REAL INK

The band-position test alone is not sufficient. Run on the real
pages it produced FALSE POSITIVES immediately, and each needed a
condition that is also measured rather than chosen:

BAND THICKNESS. A SOLID block has every column full-height and every
row full-width, so it presents exactly one vertical band and one
horizontal band whose centre is at 0.5 -- arithmetically identical to
a crossing. On EQ0192 that split ten components, including all three
equals signs (28x3 px, fill 1.00) and five 4x4 dots.

    measured band thickness / perpendicular extent
      real crossings   3/548 = 0.005   and   3/202 = 0.015
      a solid bar      1.000

    `band = 0.25` sits in that gap.

ARM ASPECT. A 5x5 dot with one 1 px column and one 1 px row passes
every band test at a 5 px extent. Each piece the split emits must
itself be rule-shaped.

    measured arm aspect
      real crossings   27, 34, 34, 67, 183
      false positives  5

    `min_aspect = 10` sits in that gap. It is BELOW `is_rule`'s 20 on
    purpose: a crossing's arm is bounded by the MATRIX, not by the
    page, so a 2x2 array's rule is legitimately short.

After both, EQ0192 splits exactly 3 components and EQ1059 exactly 1.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
3. WHAT SPLIT, AND INTO WHAT

EQ0192, source page 81 -- three `{c|c}` arrays:

      82 x 103  fill 0.065  at (117,10)   -> 1 vertical + 1 horizontal
     103 x 103  fill 0.057  at (298,10)   -> 1 vertical + 1 horizontal
      82 x 103  fill 0.065  at (453,10)   -> 1 vertical + 1 horizontal

EQ1059, source page 346 -- one `{lll|l}` array with a fourth-row rule:

     548 x 202  fill 0.020  at (140,11)   -> 1 vertical + 1 horizontal

Six rules on EQ0192 and two on EQ1059: eight in total, against eight
in the LaTeX, and `ink.rules[]` finds none of them.

BRACKETS REFUSED: 6 on EQ0192 (three arrays x two delimiters) and 2
on EQ1059. Zero wrongly split in either.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
4. A MUTATION SWEEP FOUND THE TESTS ONE LEVEL TOO SHALLOW

Worth recording because the fixtures LOOKED thorough. The first
sweep over the seven guards reported SIX SURVIVORS. None was dead
code. Every one was redundant ON MY FIXTURES: a solid 28x3 bar is
refused by the vertical band-thickness test AND the horizontal one,
so deleting either changes nothing and both read as unkilled.

The fix was a fixture per guard, each refused by exactly one:

    vertical band too thick      40 x 400, 20 px stem
    horizontal band too thick   400 x  40, 20 px bar
    vertical arm too short      400 x  20, aspect 6.7
    horizontal arm too short     20 x 400
    bands at both ends          the bracket, 40 px wide
    two vertical bands          an H -- one crossbar, two stems

Second sweep: all seven killed.

The bracket fixture had to be WIDENED from 20 px to 40 to make the
end-position guard the thing under test. At 20 px its serifs are too
short to be rules, so the arm-aspect guard refuses it first, and a
fixture that two guards both reject cannot show which one works.

The H is the case the single-vertical-band guard exists for: one
crossbar between two stems cannot be attributed to either, so the
component is refused rather than split on whichever stem comes
first. Without that guard it emits a confident wrong answer.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
5. NOT DONE

`crossing_rules` is NOT wired into `free_rules` or `ink.rules[]`.
Wiring it changes what a published key contains, and two sessions
consume this project's output -- pdfdrill-7b reads `ink.components`
and `ink.holes`, and the github.io deploy gate reads `rows` and each
row's `flag`. Adding rules to `ink.rules[]` is a change to the
instrument's answer, not to its plumbing, and it needs the premise
measurement 196 and 200 both flagged: how often a crossing occurs
across the corpus, and what else on a page has a full-span band in
one axis. Two pages is enough to build the discriminator and not
enough to turn it on.
