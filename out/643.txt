--------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IT CONFIRMS

POOR_FIT is the rule out/640 INVENTED, and it is now the only one of
the five with an external verdict on it. Its history is worth keeping
beside the result:

  * It replaced a rule I had to retract. The first R3 compared each
    row's own best-fit scale against the document's; all 9 rows it
    accused turned out to be artefacts of my own search collapsing to
    the floor of its scale range.
  * What those rows really had in common was a HIGH MARGIN WITH A LOW
    SCORE -- placed with certainty, and fitting badly. That is a
    contradiction between two computations rather than a property of
    one, which is why it was worth keeping.
  * It is now the largest red class, 12 of 15, and it is confirmed.

THE MARGIN-LED GATE IS VINDICATED TOO. out/639 concluded that the
score must be reported and never gated, because gating on it rejected
rows whose position was certain. Eleven of these fifteen have
MARGIN EQUAL TO SCORE -- the best non-overlapping rival scores ZERO,
so the expression fits in exactly one place in the line and nowhere
else at all -- while their scores run 0.56 to 0.92, well under any
plausible score threshold. A score gate would have discarded eleven
confirmed findings.

    FO0111 0.709   FO0112 0.713   FO0144 0.915   FO0150 0.820
    FO0156 0.565   FO0170 0.650   FO0173 0.715   FO0200 0.629
    FO0207 0.589   FO0228 0.681   FO0237 0.581

AND WHAT IT REFUTES -- MY OWN ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATION

Handing over the fifteen I wrote:

    "Nine of the fifteen have blob counts that match exactly. Those
    are flagged on SHAPE disagreement alone... If those look right to
    your eye, the likeliest cause is my renderer's font differing from
    the paper's, not a document error."

Seven of the twelve POOR_FIT rows have an exact blob match -- FO0096,
FO0111, FO0112, FO0154, FO0170, FO0173, FO0228 -- and the review says
the poor fit was real and visible in those images.

SO THE FONT HYPOTHESIS IS REFUTED. An exact component count with a
badly agreeing profile is not the signature of a renderer mismatch;
it is a real difference in what the glyphs are, at the same count.
That is the interesting case, not the noisy one: same number of
things, different things.

It also removes the softest reading of the rule. POOR_FIT could have
been "inkdrill and pdflatex disagree about typography"; it is not.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IS STILL NOT ESTABLISHED

  * WHICH SIDE IS WRONG. A confirmed poor fit says the rendered LaTeX
    and the ink disagree. It does not say whether MathPix mis-read the
    page or the page contains something the LaTeX cannot express. The
    evidence that would separate them -- the ink inside the rectangle
    that no rendered glyph covers -- is computable and was named in
    out/640 as unused. It still is.
  * THE OTHER THREE RULES. The verdict addresses the markings, and
    POOR_FIT explicitly. EDGE_CUT (1 row), BLOB_COUNT (3) and OVERLAP
    (2) were not separately confirmed row by row, and OVERLAP in
    particular fired for the first time in this run.
  * NO FALSE-NEGATIVE RATE. Fifteen accusations were checked; the 105
    SILENT rows were not. Nothing here says how many wrong rows sit
    among them.
  * ONE DOCUMENT, 260 of 3,163 rows, LaTeX-produced. The Infineon
    counterexample has not been run.
