645 - the refit over all 260, and the calibration retaken
==========================================================

`formulafind` now refits the scale PER ROW inside +/-12% of the
document scale. out/644 measured the case for it on fifteen rows; this
is the run over all 260 with the calibration retaken from scratch, as
out/644 said it had to be before any count was quoted.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
FIRST, THE SPEED, BECAUSE IT IS WHAT MADE THE RUN POSSIBLE

A per-row refit is 121 scales where there was one. The profile match
was a Python loop over every column at every offset; it is now two
shifts, two bitwise ops and two `bit_count()` calls on big integers,
which is the same arithmetic done in C -- the same reason the mask
encoding is `bytes.translate` and `bytes.find`.

Held to the old implementation over 300 random (template, line, scale)
triples: 0 disagreements. An optimisation that changes the answer is a
bug, and this one sits on the path the refit multiplies by 121.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
THE ALARM I HAD TO CHASE FIRST

    54 of 260 refits sit ON the band edge

My own docstring says a high count there means the band is too narrow
or the fit is degenerate, so this had to be resolved before any result
was reported. Broken down:

               n    median gaps   median score   placed
    LOW edge   48       0.0           1.000        0
    HIGH edge   6       4.0           0.800        0
    interior  206       6.0           0.958      127

THE DEGENERACY IS REAL AND LANDS ENTIRELY WHERE IT CANNOT DO HARM. All
48 low-edge rows have 3 gaps or fewer -- 28 of them have ZERO -- and
score a median of 1.000, which is the `G` failure of out/639 exactly:
a one-blob template shrinking until it fits anywhere. Every one is
already excluded by the gaps >= 2 rule.

NOT ONE OF THE 127 PLACED ROWS SITS ON EITHER EDGE. That is the number
that matters and the tool now prints that one; printing 54 of 260 was
alarming and misleading.

The 6 high-edge rows want MORE than +12% and none is placed. They are
the one thing this run leaves genuinely unexplained.

WHAT THE REFIT BOUGHT

                        one scale    refit
    rows placed             95        127
    SILENT                 105        127
    GREY                   140        115   (AMBIGUOUS 79 -> 54)
    RED                     15         18
    reference population    120        145
    reading-order pairs   12/12      18/18
    OVERLAP fires            2          0

    CALIBRATION, retaken
      score floor         0.7167    0.7854
      blob-diff spread    {0:98, 1:19, 2:1, 3:1, 6:1}
                       -> {0:128, 1:14, 2:1, 3:1, 4:1}

The floor RISES because the population fits better, which is the
honest direction: a rule calibrated on a sharper instrument is
stricter, not looser. 36 of 260 rows change class.

OVERLAP GOING TO ZERO IS THE CLEANEST RESULT HERE. out/644 diagnosed
the FO0236/FO0237 pair as one row sitting 260 px too far left. Refit
it and the overlap dissolves; both rows are now SILENT. The rule was
right, the cause was placement, and fixing the placement removed the
symptom rather than hiding it.

Five become SILENT -- FO0096, FO0144, FO0154, FO0200, FO0236 -- and

exactly as out/644 predicted they would:

    FO0200  39->38 becomes 39->39, 0.63 -> 0.83   SILENT
    FO0236  overlap dissolves                     SILENT
    FO0237  37->38 becomes 37->37, 0.58 -> 0.71   still RED, just
                                                  under the new floor
    FO0207  50->44 becomes 50->49, 0.59 -> 0.68   still RED
    FO0150  53->50 unchanged,      0.82 -> 0.84   still RED

FO0150 is the equation-number class out/644 named: `11 R(x y)=...`
with the number folded into the expression. No scale reconciles a
flush-left equation number against a thin math space, and the refit
confirms it by not moving.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
AND NINE NEW RED ROWS, WITH A PATTERN

FO0081, FO0093, FO0114, FO0115, FO0134, FO0151, FO0162, FO0172,
FO0221. These were SILENT or GREY only because a loose fit was hiding
inside a loose instrument.

A LEAD, NOT YET A FINDING. Set-builder notation is enriched among the
red rows against its own base rate in the placed population:

    set braces \{ or \left\{     RED  8/18 (44.4%)   placed 17/145 (11.7%)
    \mid                         RED  6/18 (33.3%)   placed 13/145 ( 9.0%)
    \quad                        RED  3/18 (16.7%)   placed 15/145 (10.3%)
    \operatorname                RED  2/18 (11.1%)   placed 12/145 ( 8.3%)

Braces at 3.8x and `\mid` at 3.7x; the two controls are flat, which is
what makes the first two worth looking at rather than an artefact of
long expressions being flagged more often. EIGHT ROWS IS EIGHT ROWS --
against an expected 2.1 it is well beyond chance, but it is a lead and
is recorded as one.

The mechanism to test, and NOT tested here: `\mid` takes LaTeX's
relation spacing, which is wide, and `\left\{...\right\}` sizes braces
to their content. If MathPix's reconstruction spaces the bar or sizes
the braces differently from the page, everything after the bar shifts
-- a displacement INSIDE the expression, which is exactly "placed
right, shape wrong". That is a testable prediction: the residual
should concentrate to one side of the bar.
