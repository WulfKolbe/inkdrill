646 - the \mid spacing hypothesis: supported for a third of its rows
=====================================================================

out/645 offered a lead: set-builder rows were 3.8x enriched among the
red rows, and `\mid` is a `\mathrel`, so TeX puts a thickmuskip
(0.2778em) on EACH side. If the page sets a tighter bar, everything
after it sits ~0.55em too far right in our render -- a displacement
INSIDE the expression, which is exactly "placed uniquely, shape
disagrees".

`tools/midtest.py` re-renders each row with three bars and refits:

    mid    \mid     as MathPix emits it, relation space both sides
    bar    |        \mathord, no relation space at all
    wide   \;|\;    wider than \mid, to check the DIRECTION

THE CONTROL IS THE POINT OF THE DESIGN. Thirteen placed rows contain
`\mid`: six are red and SEVEN ARE NOT. A change that also "improves"
rows which already fit is not a cause, it is a fitting artefact.

THE CONTROL BARELY MOVES AND THE RED ROWS DO. That asymmetry is the
result. A tighter bar is not a universal improvement -- it makes six
of the seven control rows WORSE, by up to 0.083 -- so the two rows it
lifts by 0.16 are not being flattered by a looser template.

TWO ROWS ARE DECISIVE. FO0112 goes 0.762 -> 0.919 and FO0115
0.767 -> 0.930. Both clear the 0.785 floor comfortably, so on this
evidence they are not document errors at all: they are our `\mid`
against the page's tighter bar.

`wide` never wins, on any row. The direction is one-way, which is what
the mechanism predicts.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
P2 -- DOES THE RESIDUAL SIT AFTER THE BAR? NO, AND THE TEST IS WEAK

Median over the rows where the bar could be located: 0.105 before,
0.121 after. Near enough equal. No general one-sided concentration.

AND THE TEST ONLY REACHED 6 OF 13 ROWS. The bar is found as the one
tall thin stroke, and in `\{A \in O(n) \mid ...\}` the braces are tall
thin strokes too, so several blobs qualify and the detector returns
nothing rather than guess. A split in the wrong place would make the
comparison meaningless, so it refuses -- but that leaves P2 tested on
half the rows and it should not be leaned on either way.

Two rows do show a strong split, both of the same shape
(`\left\{X_{i j} \mid 0 \leq i<j \leq 7\right\}`): FO0172 at 0.44
before / 0.05 after, FO0179 at 0.33 / 0.02. Both are rows the tight
bar helps. That is suggestive and it is three rows short of a finding.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
THE PLACEMENT, DRAWN

`<id>-fit-mid.png` and `<id>-fit-bar.png` composite the RENDERED
template in red over the ink in black, at the fitted scale and offset.
Where they agree the red sits on the black; where they drift, the red
walks off.

  FO0112 WITH `\mid`: the doubling is spread across the WHOLE
  expression, and the bar itself renders as a visible `||` -- our bar
  and the page's bar in two different places. The fit has compromised
  the scale to average the error out, so nothing lines up exactly.

  FO0112 WITH `|`: the left half lands crisply on the ink, single
  glyphs all the way to `O(n)`, and the entire remaining error is a
  local smear at the bar and just after it. That is the mechanism made
  visible: a wide bar does not shift one part of the expression, it
  forces a global scale compromise.

  FO0081 WITH `\mid`: the counter-example. The red is offset from the
  black more or less uniformly and the bar is single, not doubled. Its
  misfit is not the bar, which is what the +0.000 says.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THIS MEANS, AND ONE THING DELIBERATELY NOT DONE

  * `\mid` SPACING EXPLAINS A THIRD OF ITS OWN CLASS: 2 of 6 red rows
    decisively, 2 marginally, 2 not at all. It does not explain the
    other twelve red rows, which contain no bar.
  * THE FINDING IS ABOUT THE LATEX, NOT THE MATHS. The paper is
    LaTeX-produced; an author who wrote a tight bar and a
    reconstruction that writes `\mid` differ typographically and agree
    semantically. So POOR_FIT is a TRUE positive on these rows and a
    LOW-VALUE one, and a consumer wants it separated from a wrong
    subscript rather than listed beside it. That is a reason to split
    the class, not to widen the tolerance.
  * FORMULAFIND WAS NOT CHANGED TO RENDER `|`. Swapping the token
    because it scores better is tuning the instrument to the data --
    it would raise the scores of exactly the rows the instrument is
    supposed to flag, and it would do so by making our render less
    faithful to the LaTeX we were given. The whole point is to render
    what MathPix said and see whether it matches.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
NOT CLAIMED

  * SIX RED ROWS AND SEVEN CONTROLS, one document. The control is what
    makes the asymmetry meaningful and it is seven rows.
  * P2 is tested on 6 of 13 rows because the bar detector refuses
    ambiguity. It is neither supported nor refuted.
  * Whether the page really carries a tighter bar was NOT read off the
    page directly -- it is inferred from the fit improving. Reading
    the author's source from `0902.0431.tex.zip` would settle it and
    was not done.
