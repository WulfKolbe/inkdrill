621 - the grid skeleton from white space
====================================================================

THE TEST PASSES ON THE CASE IT WAS BUILT FOR. On the crop MathPix read
wrong, the white-space grid gives the right dimensions.

    case                       truth    detected   rows   cols
    clean    0212032 p7         4 x 4    4 x 4      OK     OK
    ddots    1501.00696 p3      4 x 4    6 x 4      MISS   OK
    WRONG    1006.4908 p4       1 x 16   1 x 16     OK     OK

        MathPix on that last one: 2 rows, [10, 6]
        the ink:                  1 row,  16

  Columns: 3 of 3. Rows: 2 of 3, and the miss is the `\ddots` row,
  which is why it was in the set.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE CROPS, AND HOW TRUTH WAS ESTABLISHED

Every one was cropped from its source PDF at 600 dpi through its
MathPix region, and READ BY EYE. The truth below is what the picture
shows, not what any tool said.

  CLEAN   0212032 p7, `\left[ array{cccc} \right]`
          a 4x4 of r_{i,j}. The crop also contains a line of prose
          ABOVE the matrix, which the region swallowed.

  DDOTS   1501.00696 p3, `\left( array{cccc} \right)`
          X (x) Y = a 4x4 of x_{ij}Y with \cdots across row 1-2,
          \vdots down each column and \ddots at (3,3). The crop
          contains `X (x) Y =` to the LEFT of the delimiter and a
          full stop to the right.

  WRONG   1006.4908 p4, `\left[ array{llllllllll} \right]`
          ONE row of sixteen: nu_L nu_R e_L e_R u^r_L u^r_R d^r_L
          d^r_R u^g_L u^g_R d^g_L d^g_R u^b_L u^b_R d^b_L d^b_R.
          MathPix declared ten columns and split it into two rows of
          10 and 6 -- it invented a line break after the tenth item.
          This is the one the whole task turns on.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
2. EXCLUDING THE DELIMITERS DOES TWO JOBS

A delimiter is a component nearly as tall as the matrix at the left or
right extreme, and narrow. Found without reading it:

    clean    L(148,162,175,561)   R(1064,162,1092,561)
    ddots    L(420, 25,465,474)   R(1595, 25,1641,474)
    wrong    L( 29, 32, 41,115)   R(2024, 32,2036,115)

The grid is what lies BETWEEN them, bounded vertically by their own
span -- and that second part removes what the region swallowed for
free. `clean`'s grid region starts at y=162, so the line of prose
above it is outside. `ddots`' starts at x=466, so `X (x) Y =` is
outside. Neither needed a special case.

AND A CROP WITH NO DELIMITERS IS REFUSED. 0902.0431 p14 was the first
candidate for the "wrong" slot -- MathPix reads it [3,3,3,2] against a
{ccc} spec -- but the picture is four ragged lines of an ALIGNED
DISPLAY, not a matrix, and the last line is one centred expression. So
[3,3,3,2] is not a misreading, and the detector returns

    "the delimiters enclose nothing"

rather than a 4x3 grid. Reporting a grid there would be inventing
structure, which is the failure this channel would be built to avoid.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE SEPARATORS ARE FOUND BY A BREAK, NOT BY A WIDTH

Full-height white columns inside the grid region, sorted widest first:

    clean   94  93  92 | 24  21  20  19   8   8   8   8   2 ...
    ddots  103  95  89 | 21  16   3   2
    wrong   39  39  38  38  38  37  37  36  35 ...          (no step)

The step between a column separator and the white inside a cell is
3.8x, 4.2x and 4.9x. That is a separation, not a tuned cut, and it is
the same shape 309 measured for word spaces -- two populations with a
trough between them.

TWO RULES THAT LOOK RIGHT AND ARE NOT:

  Taking the LARGEST step is wrong. `clean` has a 4.0x step at k=11,
  between two intra-cell gaps, which is later and bigger than the 3.8x
  that actually divides the columns. First step above 2x, not biggest.

  Always looking for a step is wrong. A matrix whose cells are single
  symbols has no intra-cell white at all: `clean`'s rows are 40, 40,
  39 and the answer is three separators. A rule that must find a break
  invents one and returns 3 rows where there are 4.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE \ddots ROW BREAKS ROW DETECTION, AS PREDICTED

    ddots row gaps:  59  42  22  16  16      detected 6 rows, truth 4

The `\vdots` in each column is three separated dots, and the white
BETWEEN those dots runs the full width of the grid region -- because
every column has a \vdots at the same height. So they are
indistinguishable from row separators by the full-width test, and
there is no step to find: 42/22 is 1.9 and 22/16 is 1.4, both below
the 2x the column axis clears comfortably.

IT IS NOT FIXABLE BY LOWERING THE RATIO. The true separators are the
top three of five; a 1.3x cut would take them, but 1.3x is inside the
noise the other two crops show and would split cells elsewhere. The
row axis needs something the column axis does not: a \vdots is a
COLUMN of small round components with large gaps, and recognising it
as one glyph is a component-level question, not a white-space one.

The column axis is unaffected -- \ddots and \vdots sit inside their
columns and never bridge a column separator.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
5. THE ANSWER TO THE QUESTION ASKED

  "does the detected rows x cols match what the matrix actually is?"
      2 of 3 exactly; the third is right on columns and wrong on rows.

  "on the one MathPix got wrong -- does the grid say the right
   number?"
      YES. 1 x 16 against MathPix's 2 x [10, 6]. Both axes right, on
      the case where the reader invented a row break that is not in
      the ink.

So the channel has evidence. It is worth building, with the \vdots
row as a named open problem rather than a hidden one.

WHAT THIS IS NOT. Three crops, all LaTeX-rendered arXiv papers at 600
dpi. No scan, no handwriting, no nested matrix, no `\hdotsfor` and no
spanning cell. The delimiter test assumes a bracket that is tall and
narrow; a matrix set with `\big(` on one side only, or with none,
falls to the refusal path -- correctly, but it means the channel
covers delimited matrices and nothing else.

    python3 tools/gridskel.py <crop.pgm> [--cells]
