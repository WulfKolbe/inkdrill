218 - does topology separate a slashed zero from an O?
========================================================================

ANSWER: YES, and it does more than that. The two glyphs differ by
exactly one hole, which is a difference no threshold has to be chosen
for. And on the two equations named, the topology CONTRADICTS the
labels: both final glyphs are the same slashed ring, and MathPix
called one `\emptyset` and the other `\mathbb{O}`.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE MEASUREMENT

Document: Introduction to Graph and Hypergraph Theory (Voloshin).
Measured on the report CROPS -- `report-crops/..._EQ0124.jpg` and
`..._EQ0133.jpg` -- which are MathPix's own cut of each equation, so
no region mapping stands between the label and the ink.

    EQ0124   crop 531 x 104,  38 components
    EQ0133   crop 873 x  53,  44 components

Final glyph of each, punctuation excluded:

    equation   glyph box   holes   chi   crossing strokes
    EQ0124      20 x 32      2     -1          1
    EQ0133      20 x 32      2     -1          1

The two are the same size to the pixel and the same shape to the eye:
a ring with one diagonal stroke running rim to rim.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
2. WHY THE SEPARATION NEEDS NO THRESHOLD

For a connected component, chi = 1 - holes. A ring encloses one
region, so holes = 1 and chi = 0. A stroke crossing that interior
from rim to rim divides it in two, so holes = 2 and chi = -1.

    O                 holes 1   chi  0   crossing strokes 0
    slashed zero      holes 2   chi -1   crossing strokes 1

    crossing strokes = holes - 1,  for any ring-based glyph.

That is an identity, not a measured cut. Nothing is tuned, nothing
depends on dpi or stroke weight, and the two classes cannot be
brought together by a threshold because they differ by an INTEGER.

A single-hole glyph of the same size class on the same page, for
contrast: 21 x 31, holes 1, chi 0, fill 0.37.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE CONTRADICTION -- THE ACTUAL FINDING

    equation   MathPix's label      the ink
    EQ0124     \neq \emptyset       ring + 1 crossing stroke
    EQ0133     \neq \mathbb{O}      ring + 1 crossing stroke

`\mathbb{O}` is a blackboard-bold capital O -- a ring, no crossing
stroke, holes 1, chi 0. The glyph printed in EQ0133 has TWO holes.
It is not a `\mathbb{O}`; it is the same `\emptyset` that EQ0124
carries, and the two crops agree to the pixel.

So the topology does not merely separate the classes in principle --
on this pair it detects a specific wrong label, from ink alone,
without naming a single symbol. That is what this project is for: the
residual is the product.

The direction matters too. A tool that returns `\mathbb{O}` where the
page reads a slashed zero produces LaTeX that compiles, renders a
plausible glyph, and is wrong. Nothing downstream of the conversion
can catch it, because nothing downstream sees the ink.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
4. ONE MEASUREMENT THAT SURPRISED ME, RECORDED BECAUSE IT WOULD
   OTHERWISE BE ASSUMED

The `\neq` preceding each glyph has ZERO holes:

    EQ0124   26 x 35   holes 0   chi 1
    EQ0133   25 x 35   holes 0   chi 1

Two horizontal bars with a diagonal through them would be expected to
enclose two regions. They do not, because the bars OVERHANG the
diagonal at both ends, so the would-be regions are open at the sides
and connect to the background. A reader predicting the topology of
`\neq` from its shape would have written 2 and been wrong.

That is worth stating beside the `\emptyset` result: the same
reasoning that gets the slashed zero right gets `\neq` wrong, and the
difference is whether the strokes CLOSE. Only measurement settles it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
5. WHAT THIS DOES NOT SAY

- NOT that holes = 2 identifies `\emptyset`. Other glyphs have two
  holes -- `8`, `B`, `theta` with a crossbar, several script capitals
  on these very pages. The topology separates a slashed ring FROM an
  unslashed one; it does not name either.
- NOT a corpus rate. Two equations, one document, chosen by the task.
  How often MathPix substitutes `\mathbb{O}` for `\emptyset` is a
  different measurement and is not taken here.
- NOT that the crossing stroke is always detectable. It divides the
  interior only if it reaches the rim on both sides. A short slash
  that stops inside the ring leaves one hole and is invisible to this
  test -- the same closure question section 4 raises for `\neq`.
