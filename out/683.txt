683 - paragraph boundaries from ink, against the authors' own \par
=================================================================

pdfdrill handed this over with a number: its reader infers 132
paragraphs on arXiv 1102.1889 where the author's source has 332. The
question behind it is this project's kind of question -- is a paragraph
boundary IN THE INK, before anything is read? -- so the instrument had
to come first, and most of this task went into it.

    measure.py paragraphs --doc sigma26        (the 34th subcommand)

====================================================================
1. THE INSTRUMENT, AND THE THREE DEFECTS IT HAD TO LOSE FIRST
====================================================================

GOLD IS THE AUTHOR'S OWN `\par` STRUCTURE, read from the `.tex` beside
the PDF -- not another tool's opinion. It is placed on the page through
the reader's words, because nothing else joins a source file to ink, and
THAT JOIN IS MEASURED: a gold paragraph that cannot be located is
reported, never dropped in silence.

Each of the three defects below changed the answer. Each was found by
the measurement disagreeing with the page, not by a test.

A DISPLAY IS NOT A PARAGRAPH BREAK. The first gold broke a paragraph
wherever a display equation OPENED. In LaTeX `text \begin{equation}
... \end{equation} text` is ONE paragraph and its continuation is set
unindented.

    gold, three documents      461  ->  382      (-17%)

The tell was not in the source. It was that the median indent AT A GOLD
BOUNDARY came out at 0.02 line heights -- the measurement was reporting
that paragraph starts are not indented, which is not true of LaTeX. A
gold that says that is not describing the document.

AN ANCHOR MUST BE WORDS THE PAGE CAN CARRY. Anchoring on a paragraph's
first 40 characters located 27% of the gold, because `\cite{ACM}` prints
as `[1]`, `\ref{s:2}` as `2` and `\begin{definition}` as `Definition
2.1.`. Anchoring on the first gap-free RUN between such cuts:

    located                     27%  ->  90%     (three documents)

A REPEATED RUN IS NOT A LOCATION. `str.find` returns the first
occurrence, and on this corpus that is often the abstract, which repeats
a sentence of the body: a paragraph on page 9 was being located on
page 1. Now counted and dropped (284 of 2,954 over the twenty).

A BOX TOP IS NOT A LINE. The reader's region top includes the leading
and sits above the ink, so matching a boundary by its top edge credited
it to the line ABOVE. One crop settled it -- sigma26-073 p5, where the
indented `Conversely,` line is band 38 and the gold had landed on 37.

    median indent at a gold boundary   0.02  ->  1.32 line heights

Three instrument defects, and the first and last each produced the SAME
false conclusion: "paragraph starts are not indented". It would have
been quotable, reproducible and wrong.

====================================================================
2. THE MEASUREMENT
====================================================================

Two channels, both from ink alone, both in units of THE PAGE'S OWN
median band height -- a threshold in page pixels is silently retuned by
a dpi change, so the normalisation and the constant live in one
function:

    INDENT  leftmost ink is 1.0 to 3.0 band heights right of the body
            margin. A WINDOW, not a floor: a centred display is
            indented too, by much more.
    GAP     the band sits >= 0.3 band heights further below its
            predecessor than the page's median pitch.

POPULATION: 20 SIGMA documents (Symmetry, Integrability and Geometry),
531 pages carrying at least one located boundary, 22,100 ink bands,
2,954 gold paragraphs of which 2,247 (76.2%) could be located and put in
a band. SPLIT RULE: list environments do NOT count as paragraphs
(`--par-lists` flips it); front matter and paragraphs under 40
characters are dropped, 190 and 294 of them, printed beside the result.

    channel                         recall   precision    (prose bands)
    indent in [1.0, 3.0]             52.4%      56.2%
    gap >= 0.3                       54.0%      37.5%
    indent OR gap                    86.5%      42.9%
    indent OR (gap, not after a display)
                                     66.4%      51.1%

"Prose bands" are the bands the reader types as text or list_item --
the reader is used to RESTRICT THE POPULATION and to break the residual
down, never as a channel. Over every band, unrestricted, the union is
85.8% at 31.7%.

====================================================================
3. THE RESIDUAL IS THE PRODUCT: THE POST-DISPLAY BREAK
====================================================================

A gap below a display, with no indent, on a prose band:

    421   ARE a paragraph boundary
   1081   are the same paragraph continuing

Ink geometry cannot separate them, and neither can the gap: the vertical
space belongs to the display, not to the break. The indent is the only
signal that distinguishes them, and in exactly this case LaTeX does not
set one -- a continuation after a display is unindented, and so is a new
paragraph whose author wrote `\noindent`, and so is the first paragraph
after a heading.

That is where the under-segmentation pdfdrill measured comes from, and
it is not a threshold that needs tuning. 1,586 displays were spanned in
these twenty documents; every one of them is a place where the page
stops saying what the author said.

PRECISION IS UNDERSTATED AND CANNOT BE CORRECTED HERE. 23.8% of gold
could not be located, so every rule hit on an unlocatable boundary is
counted as a false positive. The ceiling is not known; what is known is
that the measured precision is a floor.

====================================================================
WHAT THIS DOES NOT SAY
====================================================================
  * ONE PUBLISHER, until section 4. Everything above is SIGMA, one
    journal class, one `\parindent`. See section 4 -- it changes which
    channel matters.
  * Pages are those carrying at least one LOCATED boundary (531 of the
    597 rendered), so a page whose every anchor was lost is absent.
  * Nothing here segments a document. It measures whether the boundary
    is VISIBLE in ink; emitting paragraphs is not built.
  * The gold is the author's `\par`, not the typeset reality: an author
    who writes a blank line before `\noindent` gets a gold boundary the
    page deliberately does not show.

====================================================================
4. THE TRANSFER CHECK -- AND WHAT DID NOT TRANSFER
====================================================================

The library holds 1,291 documents carrying both LaTeX source and a
render. 20 of them sampled at `--seed 683`, 190 rendered pages, 1,961
gold paragraphs, 81.5% located:

    channel                         SIGMA (20)    mixed, 1-column (13)
    indent in [1.0, 3.0]          52.4% / 56.2%     27.7% / 46.2%
    gap >= 0.3                    54.0% / 37.5%     70.0% / 63.8%
    indent OR gap                 86.5% / 42.9%     88.2% / 57.9%

THE UNION TRANSFERS; THE CHANNELS DO NOT. Recall of the union is within
two points across two unrelated publishers, while the channel carrying
it swaps over: SIGMA indents and barely spaces, the mixed sample spaces
and barely indents. A rule tuned on either channel alone would have been
retuned by the next publisher, and would have looked well-founded both
times.

TWO COLUMNS BREAK THE INSTRUMENT, AND IT DOES NOT DEGRADE GRACEFULLY.
Of the 20, seven are two-column, and recall separates COMPLETELY -- no
overlap between the groups:

    single column   13 documents, 770 boundaries   median 92.9%  (75.0-100)
    two columns      7 documents, 331 boundaries   median 18.8%  ( 7.7-25.9)

The cause is not a threshold. `_par_bands` takes a row profile across the
whole page, so on a two-column page a band spans BOTH columns and is not
a line at all: the body margin becomes one column's, every line of the
other reads as indented past the window, and the leading of two
independent columns interleaves. The measurement now reports the column
class per document and splits the channel table by it, because one
number over a mixed corpus averages a population the instrument can see
with one it cannot.

The post-display residual also depends on the corpus, and in the
direction the mechanism predicts: SIGMA is a maths journal and spans
1,586 displays, where the gap-after-display case is 421 boundaries
against 1,081 continuations; the mixed sample spans 340, and there the
same case is 214 against 123.

A FIFTH DEFECT, IN THE SPLIT RULE ITSELF. The first column estimate
counted every body-width line that starts mid-page -- and a CENTRED
DISPLAY EQUATION is exactly that. It called sigma26-085, a one-column
maths paper, two-column on 16 equations and one sentence, and then filed
its 93.3% recall under the heading for documents the instrument cannot
see. A split rule that misfiles a result as "unmeasurable" hides it as
thoroughly as a filter that excludes the class it exists to compare
against. The estimate now counts only the lines the reader types as
prose, and the case is pinned in `tests/test_paragraph_gold.py`.

Both populations were re-run after that fix. SIGMA is unchanged to the
decimal (86.5% / 42.9%, residual 421 against 1,081) and no SIGMA
document is two-column; the mixed sample's groups did not move either.

====================================================================
WHAT SECTION 4 DOES NOT SAY
====================================================================

  * 20 documents against 1,291 eligible. The column split is clean on
    this sample; it is not a claim about the library.
  * The two-column figure measures THE BAND MODEL, not the page. A
    column-aware band would have to be built and measured before
    anything is concluded about two-column paragraphs.
  * `--par-lists False` throughout: list items are not paragraphs here.
    The flag exists because that choice changes the answer and a reader
    must be able to change it back.

====================================================================
5. WHAT WOULD FIX THE TWO-COLUMN CASE, MEASURED
====================================================================

Assumption 10 says a row profile is a text line and that this is false
on two columns. The complementary profile says where to cut. Longest
interior blank run of the VERTICAL ink profile, as a fraction of the
inked body width, over 30 pages whose column class was labelled
independently (by where the reader's prose lines start):

    1 column, 16 pages   0.00 on every one -- not small, exactly zero
    2 columns, 14 pages  0.00 0.00 0.07 0.50 0.53 1.18 1.77
                         4.21 4.35 4.39 4.41 4.41 4.41 4.45

The populations do not touch, so a cut has a whole gap to sit in rather
than an edge to balance on. Two things it must not be:

  * NOT 4.5%. That is the value a clean two-column page SHOWS, and it
    is the maximum here -- a cut there is above the entire class it
    exists to detect. The middle of the gap is about 2%.
  * NOT per document. 4 of the 14 two-column pages have NO gutter and
    three more are under 1.8%, because a figure or table spans the
    break. On those pages banding the whole width is correct, and a
    document-level answer is wrong exactly where it matters.

This is pdfdrill's constant (their 848), re-measured here on 30 pages
against their two, because inkdrill holds the renders and an
independent label. The column-aware band is NOT built in inkdrill;
what this records is that the signal is there and where the cut goes.

A CORPUS FINDING, not a result: several library documents carry
byte-identical duplicate renders under both spellings -- `p5.png` AND
`page-0005.png` (bommes_zimmer 3+3, 2306.04271 9+9, p1713-suchanek
2+2, Cat2Type 3+3; the sigma26 shelf is clean). Anything addressing a
page by name is unaffected; anything globbing `*.png` double-counts,
and the first cut of the measurement above did until it de-duplicated.
