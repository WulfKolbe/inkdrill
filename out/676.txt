676 - anti-aliasing: off already, now said out loud -- and two of the books are scans
====================================================================================

A side question from the user: do our ghostscript calls turn
anti-aliasing off (`-dTextAlphaBits=1 -dGraphicsAlphaBits=1`, before the
input file)? Checked rather than assumed, because every correlation in
the formula work compares a RENDERED template with a RENDERED page.

====================================================================
1. THE ANSWER: OFF BY DEVICE DEFAULT, AND NOW BY OUR OWN SAYING
====================================================================

`formulafind.render` writes `pgmraw` and passed no alpha flags. The
output is BYTE-IDENTICAL with and without them:

    300 dpi   default c2a2be18...  explicit `=1` c2a2be18...  SAME
              anti-aliased `=4` 284d7644...                   differs
    600 dpi   default 1d13b050...  explicit `=1` 1d13b050...  SAME
              anti-aliased `=4` ad8ba1cc...                   differs
    gs 10.07.1

By level count on one rendered formula: default and `=1` both give 5
grey levels with 3 mid-tone pixels in the whole image; `=4` gives 18
levels and 1,075 mid-tone pixels.

THE FLAGS ARE NOW PASSED ANYWAY. The default belongs to the DEVICE, not
to us -- `pngalpha` anti-aliases by default -- and a template rendered
with grey edges correlates differently against a hard-edged page. The
change cannot move a measurement: the bytes are the same today.

Deliberate exceptions, left alone: `scriptink.py` and two calls in
`premise/measure.py` ask for `=4` on purpose, and `dpialias.py` exists to
compare aliased against anti-aliased.

====================================================================
2. WHAT THE PAGES WE MEASURE ACTUALLY CONTAIN
====================================================================

The crops come from `inspect/pages/p<N>.png`, pdfdrill's renders, not
ours. Five pages sampled per document over the 21 published books, grey
levels and the share of mid-tone pixels (10 < v < 245):

    two-level ink, as expected      0902.0431, 1510.06699, 2010.14265,
                                    2106.07890, 2501.06662, kohlhase-omdoc,
                                    bradley_spring22, voloshin, lyche,
                                    cardona, mielke, gilmore (2-4 levels
                                    away from their cover page)
    GREY ON EVERY SAMPLED PAGE      penev_A (84-95 levels), penev_B (93-96)
    grey on some pages              0707.4470, 1605.05775, fong-spivak x2,
                                    johnston, rph8-g15q, BradleyGastaldi

    26 of 105 sampled pages carry real grey (> 16 levels)

penev_A and penev_B are SCANS, not renders of a digital PDF; several
other documents' first pages are scanned covers, and single interior
pages carry embedded raster figures. So "inkdrill measures two-level
ink" is true of most of this corpus and FALSE for those two books --
their crops reach `binarize` as real greyscale, and the threshold does
the work.

====================================================================
WHAT THIS DOES NOT SAY
====================================================================

  * 5 pages per document, evenly spaced, 105 of ~2,900 -- a sample, not
    a census; the per-document verdicts above are from those 5.
  * Nothing here re-opens HANDOVER item 11: anti-aliasing ON took hole
    instability from 75.0% to 9.4% on the RENDERED column, and that is
    still measured, still not acted on, and still about a different
    pipeline (pdfdrill's page renders, not our templates).
  * No measurement changed. The byte-identity is the whole point: this
    is a guard against a device default, not a correction.
