========================================================================

RAN. 3,163 rows, 4 shards, 13 minutes wall clock (17:47:37 -> 18:00:41)
against a predicted 9. `--shard I/N` and `--scale` were added so every
shard imposes the SAME document scale, since a per-shard vote would
have given four different ones.

The cost prediction held in shape: 0.63-0.70 s per row, FLAT with
expression length across a 10x range, because `pdflatex` + `gs` is
0.40 s of it and the bigint refit only 0.10-0.14 s. I had expected the
refit to dominate and scale. It does neither.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
AND THEN THE RESULT THAT MATTERS, WHICH IS AGAINST MY OWN INSTRUMENT

Crossing the source-side verdict against the image-side class over
3,096 rows looked like a strong finding:

    source          %RED
    matched          4.4%     (LaTeX identical to the author's)
    known class     13.8%     (LaTeX differs by a known substitution)

3.1x. Rows whose LaTeX MathPix changed are three times as likely to be
flagged in the image. Exactly what the whole chain predicts.

IT IS ENTIRELY A LENGTH CONFOUND.

    %RED         matched        known class
    0-19 ch   1.0% (n=573)     0.5% (n=205)
    20-39     4.8% (n=269)     4.2% (n=602)
    40-79    15.0% (n=100)    13.7% (n=737)
    80-999   42.1% (n=19)     39.8% (n=334)

WITHIN EVERY LENGTH BUCKET THE TWO ARE THE SAME, and where they differ
at all it is `matched` that is HIGHER. The substituted rows are longer,
that is all. Length drives the flag rate from 1% to 42% and the source
verdict adds nothing on top of it.

SO THE RED FLAGS DO NOT DETECT LATEX/SOURCE DIVERGENCE. A row whose
LaTeX is character-identical to what the author wrote is flagged at the
same rate as one MathPix rewrote, once both are the same length.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THAT MEANS, STATED PLAINLY

The eye review in out/643 confirmed the poor fits are REAL -- visible
in the image, and the reviewer's five diagnoses were all correct. That
still stands. But "a real poor fit" and "a document error" are not the
same claim, and this measurement separates them: the fit degrades with
LENGTH whatever the LaTeX says.

The most likely reading is that the residual is cumulative rendering
divergence -- our pdflatex against the paper's typesetting, a fraction
of a percent per glyph, adding up over 80 characters until the profile
match falls below a floor calibrated on shorter rows. out/646 saw a
piece of this and called it a `\mid` effect; out/648 saw another piece
and called it a prescript effect. Both were real and both were small,
and neither is the mechanism.

THIS IS TESTABLE AND WAS NOT TESTED. If the cause is cumulative, the
score of a row should fall with length on rows whose LaTeX MATCHES the
author exactly -- there the only difference left is the rendering. The
`matched` column above says 1.0% -> 42.1% over four buckets, which is
consistent with it, and consistent is not measured.
