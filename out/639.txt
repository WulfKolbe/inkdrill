639 - locating a col-5 expression inside its col-6 line crop
=============================================================

REPORT ONLY. `tools/formulafind.py` is the harness; nothing was added
to the package. The question was what inkdrill can say about the
evidence-formula table and how to show it, and the first step asked
for was a rectangle plus the matching conditions.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THE TWO COLUMNS ACTUALLY ARE

Measured on 0902.0431, `evidence-formula.tex`, 3,163 rows parsed.

  col 5  `\FitMath{$\displaystyle ...$}` -- the expression as TeX sets
         it. Rendered here by pdflatex at a dpi we choose.
  col 6  `\includegraphics{report-crops-b/<id>.jpg}` -- a crop of the
         ORIGINAL page. 3,170 FO crops; width median 741 px, height
         median 27 px (p25 25, p75 30).

The scale between them is NOT given and had to be established:

    Mathpix page raster        2125 x 2750 px  (612 pt page = 250 dpi)
    line region, lines.json    1287 x 45 px at (462, 900)
    the crop                    772 x 27 px
    crop / region              0.5998

So the crop is the line region downscaled to 60%, about 150 dpi, and a
line is 27 px tall. Rendering col 5 at 600 dpi makes the expected
ratio 150/600 = 0.250. THE MEASUREMENT FOUND 0.250 WITHOUT BEING TOLD:
63 rows voted, 28 of them exactly 0.250 and the bulk within 0.24-0.26.

At 27 px a glyph is 8-10 px tall and its counters are 1-2 px, which is
the speckle floor out/591 measured. HOLE COUNTS ARE NOT USED HERE and
that is deliberate, not an omission.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
THE METHOD, AND WHY IT IS BLOBS AND NOT PIXELS

Both masks are swept and reduced to their COMPONENTS. Each is then
turned into a binary ink/gap profile along x built from the blob
BOXES, never from the pixels: a column inside a blob's box counts as
ink even where that glyph has a gap. That is what survives a 4x scale
change and a different rasteriser.

The expression's profile is resampled to a candidate scale and slid
across the line's. The rectangle is the best offset; the y-extent is
taken from the crop blobs whose centre falls inside it.

THE SCALE IS ESTIMATED ONCE PER DOCUMENT, not per row. It is a
property of how the report was built -- one downscale applied to every
line. Estimating it per row is what made the first version wrong: it
voted 0.165 instead of 0.250, because expressions of one or two blobs
fit degenerately at ANY small scale and there are far more of them
than there are long ones. Only rows with >= 6 gaps may vote.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
IT WORKS, AND THE CHECK IS FALSIFIABLE

FO0001 and FO0002 are two different expressions in the SAME line:

    "Lie algebras, called A_n, B_n, C_n, D_n (classical type) and
     G_2, F_4, E_6, E_7, E_8 (exceptional"

    FO0001  A_{n}, B_{n}, C_{n}, D_{n}      x 177-310, y 5-23
    FO0002  G_{2}, F_{4}, E_{6}, E_{7}, E_{8}  x 496-651, y 5-23

Disjoint, in the right order, and drawn on the crop they sit exactly
on the two expressions. That check needs no ground truth, which is
just as well: the corpus has line-level geometry in `lines.json` and
in the pdfminer file, but NO per-character boxes, so there is nothing
to score a rectangle against directly.

ONE VISIBLE RESIDUAL. FO0002's right edge clips the final subscript by
about 2 px. The right edge is systematically the weaker one, because
the last thing in these expressions is usually a subscript -- small,
low, and the first thing to be lost to the 27 px raster.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
THE FIGURE OF MERIT IS THE MARGIN, NOT THE SCORE

Score is the 1-D Jaccard of the two profiles: columns where both say
ink over columns where either does. Margin is that score minus the
best score at any NON-OVERLAPPING position.

200 rows, acceptance by gap count -- the number of interior gaps in
the expression's own profile, which is its discriminating power:

    gaps   rows  accepted   rate   med score   med margin
       0     23         0    0.0%      1.000        0.000
       1     26         0    0.0%      1.000        0.000
     2-3     29         6   20.7%      0.923        0.056
     4-5     27         7   25.9%      0.871        0.071
     6-9     30        11   36.7%      0.818        0.079
   10-14     28         7   25.0%      0.736        0.091
     15+     37        13   35.1%      0.734        0.702

READ THE TWO MIDDLE COLUMNS AGAINST EACH OTHER. The score FALLS as the
expression gets longer, 1.000 down to 0.734, while the margin RISES,
0.000 up to 0.702. They move in opposite directions, and only one of
them is measuring what we want.

A PERFECT SCORE IS EVIDENCE OF FAILURE. 39 of 200 rows score exactly
1.000, and 36 of those have one gap or none. A one-blob expression's
profile is a solid bar, and a bar fits over any bar: `G` scored 1.000
at column 2 of a line it does not begin. `w` likewise. The rows that
fit perfectly are exactly the rows that carry no information about
where they are.

So the score cannot be the gate. Measured over the same 200 rows:

    gaps>=2 AND score>=0.80 AND margin>=0.10     44   (as first run)
    gaps>=2 AND margin>=0.10                     72
    gaps>=2 AND margin>=0.15                     55
    gaps>=4 AND margin>=0.10                     64
    gaps>=2, no other condition                 151

The score threshold rejects 28 rows the margin accepts, and some of
them are the strongest matches in the set -- a margin EQUAL to the
score means the runner-up scored zero, i.e. the expression fits in
exactly one place and nowhere else at all:

    FO0039  score 0.633  margin 0.633   z\left(E_{6}\right)
    FO0045  score 0.402  margin 0.402   S U(3)) / \boldsymbol{Z}_{3}
    FO0012  score 0.724  margin 0.613   \mathfrak{g}_{2}{ }^{C}

--------------------------------------------------------------------
THE MATCHING CONDITIONS, AS MEASURED

  1. SCALE is one number per document, estimated from rows with >= 6
     gaps and imposed on the rest. 0.250 here, from 63 votes.
  2. GAPS >= 2. Below that the expression cannot be placed by this
     signal at all -- 0 of 49 rows with <= 1 gap, and their scores are
     perfect.
  3. MARGIN >= 0.10 over the best non-overlapping alternative. This is
     the gate.
  4. SCORE is REPORTED, NOT GATED. Its value is diagnostic: a low
     score with a high margin means "here and nowhere else, but the
     fit is loose", which is a different finding from "nowhere".
  5. BLOB AGREEMENT as a secondary check. Among 44 accepted, the
     rendered expression's blob count equals the count inside the
     rectangle in 33 (75%); the differences are
     {-3:1, -2:1, -1:7, 0:33, +1:1, +3:1}. The -1s are glyphs that
     merge at 27 px. A large positive difference means the rectangle
     swallowed a neighbour.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT INKDRILL CAN PROVIDE HERE, AND HOW TO SHOW IT

  * A RECTANGLE per row, in crop pixel coordinates, with a margin and
    a score. That is a seventh column in the evidence table, or an
    overlay drawn on the sixth.
  * THE RESIDUAL, which is the point. Three classes fall out of the
    measurement and each is a different finding about the source:
      - PLACED     margin high: the expression is where the table says
                   it is.
      - AMBIGUOUS  gaps >= 2 but margin low: the line repeats itself
                   and the position is not established.
      - UNPLACEABLE gaps <= 1: too short to carry a position.
    An overlay makes all three visible at a glance, in three colours,
    which a single accuracy number would have thrown away.
  * BLOB-COUNT DISAGREEMENT inside the rectangle, which localises
    where the 27 px raster merged or split glyphs.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
NOT CLAIMED

  * ONE DOCUMENT. 0902.0431 only, 200 of its 3,163 rows. The scale
    0.5998 and the 250 dpi page are that report's, and the Infineon
    counterexample has not been tried.
  * NO GROUND TRUTH. The rectangles are checked by construction --
    disjointness, order, and by eye on the overlay -- not scored
    against known character boxes, because the corpus has none.
  * SCORE IS NOT REFINED PER ROW. A single document scale leaves a
    residual that accumulates across a long expression, which is the
    likeliest reason the score falls with length. Allowing +/- 2%
    per row is the obvious next measurement and was not run.
  * THE RIGHT EDGE IS WEAKER THAN THE LEFT, seen on FO0002 and not yet
    quantified.
