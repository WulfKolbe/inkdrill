655 - the conjunction validates on a second document, blind
============================================================

1510.06699, 1,062 evidence rows, 485 placed. Reviewed BLIND: eleven
rule-flagged rows and nine controls drawn at random from placed rows
the rule did NOT flag, shuffled together, the assignment hidden until
after the verdicts were given.

  FOUR WRONG OF TWENTY, AND ALL FOUR WERE FLAGGED.

    flagged   11 rows   4 wrong   precision 36.4%
                                  Wilson 95% CI [15%, 65%]
    controls   9 rows   0 wrong   no false negative in the sample

    in-sample on 0902.0431 was 41.2%, which is inside that interval.

THE CONJUNCTION HOLDS OUT OF SAMPLE. That was the open question of
out/654 -- 41.2% was measured on the same seven errors the rule was
chosen to capture, and could not be checked further on that document
because all seventeen of its rows were already labelled.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY THE CONTROLS WERE THERE, AND WHAT THEY BOUGHT

Knowing a row is flagged makes it easier to find fault with it. More
importantly, without controls a false negative costs the rule nothing:
every previous review here looked only at rows something had already
flagged, so recall has never been measured, only extrapolated.

Nine controls is a thin instrument -- it would miss an error rate
below about 10% -- but it is the first direct evidence that the
conjunction is not simply blind. Zero of nine.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
THE METHOD TRANSFERS. THE CONSTANTS DO NOT, AND SHOULD NOT

The floor was REFITTED on this document's own author-matched rows:

    1510.06699   floor(L) = 1.0146 - 0.1097*log10(L)
    0902.0431    floor(L) = 1.0806 - 0.1685*log10(L)

Shallower -- this document's rendering diverges less with length.
Reusing the first document's constants would have tested nothing. The
scale was voted for rather than assumed too, and returned 0.67 against
a predicted 0.6667.

What each signal flags, of 485 placed rows:

    per-length floor            99   20.4%
    edge cuts >= 1, gaps <= 7  137   28.2%
    CONJUNCTION                 11    2.3%

Same shape as the first document, where the figures were 75, 498 and
17 of 2,225. Each signal alone flags a fifth to a quarter of the
document; together they collapse to a page.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
A SUB-PATTERN, CHECKED ON BOTH DOCUMENTS BEFORE BEING WRITTEN DOWN

    cuts >= 2 AND gaps <= 2

    1510.06699   3 rows, 3 wrong   (FO0080, FO0551, FO0913)
    0902.0431    1 row,  1 wrong   (FO1432)
    both         4 of 4 wrong

The remaining eight flagged rows of document 2 hold one error between
them. So within the conjunction there is a hard core: two or more edge
cuts on an expression with two gaps or fewer has been wrong every time
it has been seen.

FOUR ROWS IS FOUR ROWS, and the pattern was noticed on document 2
before being checked on document 1 rather than after -- which is why
it is here as a lead with its provenance stated, and not as a rule.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
THE FAILURE MODE IS DIRECTIONAL, AND THAT IS THE REAL FINDING

Twelve labelled errors across both documents. SEVEN of them place the
expression to the LEFT of the truth, or take the thing immediately
before it:

    FO0512  the shorter word before the expression was taken
    FO0651  placed too far left
    FO1316  placed one term before the true position
    FO2157  matched half the word BEFORE the target
    FO0080  the wrong expression to the left
    FO0551  moved left, covering only half the expression
    FO0913  range moved left, covering half the expression

Against five that are anything else -- one placed slightly right, one
right edge short, one at the centre of the line, one at the end
instead of the start, one simply elsewhere.

THIS IS A BIAS, NOT NOISE. A matcher that failed at random would
scatter both ways. Seven-one on the directional errors says the
profile match prefers a position slightly left of the true one, which
is a mechanism that can be found and fixed rather than a threshold
that can only be tuned.

The likely cause, NOT TESTED: the trimmed template starts at its first
ink column, so its leading edge aligns naturally with the trailing
edge of whatever precedes the expression, and the inter-word gap on
the left is available to absorb error while the right side has to fit
real glyphs.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
WHERE THIS LEAVES THE RESIDUAL REPORT

Two documents, 155 eye verdicts, 12 labelled errors.

  ADOPT: the conjunction. 36-41% precision, 11-17 rows per document,
  against a base rate under 1%. Recall is unmeasured beyond "nine
  controls were clean".

  DO NOT ADOPT: either signal alone. The floor alone is 9%, the rule
  alone 1.7%, and both flag a fifth of the document.

  FIX FIRST: the left bias. Seven of twelve errors share one
  direction, and no threshold change will address that.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
NOT CLAIMED

  * TWO DOCUMENTS, both arXiv, both LaTeX-produced.
  * ELEVEN FLAGGED ROWS is a small denominator; the interval runs
    from 15% to 65%.
  * NINE CONTROLS cannot see a false-negative rate below ~10%.
  * The sub-pattern rests on four rows.
  * The left-bias mechanism is a hypothesis. It predicts that
    trimming the template symmetrically, or scoring the two edges
    separately, would move those seven rows -- neither was tried.
  * Nothing was adopted in code. `formularesidual` still runs the
    single floor and `edge_cut_max` at p90.
